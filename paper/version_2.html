Specification · version 2

The nested basket model, as the code implements it

Version 1 described a model the code did not fit. This version is written from scripts/model/27_nested_basket.py and scripts/model/28_nested_counterfactual.py rather than from its predecessor. Every equation states what those files compute, including the parts that are inelegant. Where the implementation departs from the mathematics it is supposed to implement, the departure is stated in place and its size is measured, not estimated.

How to read this document

Three kinds of claim are distinguished throughout and never blended. Derived — follows from stated assumptions, with the derivation given. Imposed — a modelling choice with no derivation behind it, marked in amber. Measured — a number produced by a named script, quoted with the quantity it came from.

Anything that is none of the three is not in this document.

Part IIThe systematic terms

4Product value, with and without a basket

One function is evaluated everywhere, in NestedModel.item_utility. It takes a context vector and returns

uijt(ctx) = λj + θi⊤αj + αj⊤ctx − (γi⊤βj) Δlog pjst + ψj⊤xijt + wloyjωij + wfrqjfij + wdspjDjsw + wmlrjMjsw + μj⊤δw + ζj⊤ξs
(4)

Two names are used for two evaluations of the same function, and the distinction matters everywhere below:

bijt := uijt(0)       u−jm := uimt(ᾱ−j)
(4a)

b is the value of a product with no basket around it, used wherever the basket does not yet exist. u−jm is the value of candidate m in the position currently held by j, where

ᾱ−j = 1n − 1 ∑k ∈ S, k ≠ j αk   (and ᾱ−j := 0 when n = 1),     A−j = 𝒞s(c(j)) \ (S \ {j})
(4b)

Superscript is the position, subscript the candidate. Every candidate for one position is scored against the same ᾱ−j, because the other n−1 occupants are fixed while that position is considered. Note j ∈ A−j: the incumbent competes for its own seat.

4.1The persistence terms

θi⊤αj is a rank-K bilinear form standing in for an N × J table. It can express "this household likes this kind of product" but not "this household buys this product and no other". Two scalars per (household, product) supply the difference. With n𝒯ij the number of training trips on which household i bought product j:

ωij = n𝒯ij max( 1, ∑j′ ∈ 𝒞(c(j)) n𝒯ij′ ),     fij = log(1 + n𝒯ij)log 100,     fic = log(1 + ∑j′ ∈ 𝒞(c) n𝒯ij′)log 100
(5)

ωij is exactly the quantity Eq. 12 normalises, so ∑j∈𝒞(c)ωij = 1 for any category the household has bought from and 0 otherwise — verified to float precision for 100% of (household, category) pairs by eval/33_verify_equations.py. The denominator counts purchase lines, not trips, which is what makes it a proper share.

4.2Category value

IVict = log ∑j ∈ 𝒞s(c) ebijt
(6)
ηict = c0c + cu⊤i ccc + cs⊤c xict + chabc fic + κc ( ÎVict − IVc )
(7)

IV is built from b, not from u, because category entry is decided before any product is placed and no basket context exists yet. ÎV is the estimator of Eq. 6 defined in §11 — the hat is load-bearing and §11 measures what it costs. IVc is a per-category constant frozen during fitting (§11.3). κc = softplus(κrawc) = log(1 + eκrawc), which is strictly positive for every real κraw.

4.3The two count rates

zbrdict = cbc + hi − cpc Δlog pct,      zqtyijt = aj − (γq⊤iβqj) Δlog pjst + ψq⊤j xijt
(8)

Δlog pct is the mean of Δlog p over the products of category c the store stocks. The rates are Λbrd = exp zbrd and Λqty = exp zqty.

The implementation clamps all three linear predictors

Before exponentiating, the code applies η ← clip(η, −12, 4), zqty ← clip(zqty, −6, 4) and zbrd ← clip(zbrd, −6, 3). These are numerical guards, but they are part of the fitted model: they bound P(yc=1) to [6.1×10−6, 1 − 10−24], the units rate to [0.0025, 54.6] and the breadth rate to [0.0025, 20.1]. No claim in this document depends on a clamp being inactive, and how often each binds is not currently measured.

Part IIIThe distribution

5The joint

P( ℬ ∣ 𝒢, n ≥ 1 ) = ∏c=1C P(yc) · ∏c : yc=1 P(kc ∣ yc=1) · P(S ∣ 𝒦) · ∏j ∈ S P(qj ∣ S) 1 − ∏c=1C P(yc = 0)
(9)

A trip is in the data only because something was bought. The unconditional product assigns ∏cP(yc=0) = exp(−∑ceηict) > 0 to the empty basket, which is never observed; the denominator removes it. Writing Rv = ∑c eηict, the correction adds log(1 − e−Rv) to the log-likelihood per trip.

The four factors are ordered by what each conditions on: composition first, because IV is a function of b alone; then contents given the composition; then units given the contents. This is a factorisation of one joint, not a sequence of separate models, and every factor shares the parameters of Eq. 4.

6Category entry

Each category draws an independent shock from the minimum-form Gumbel, whose CDF is F(x) = 1 − exp(−ex), and is entered when the shock falls below its value:

εc ~ Gumbelmin(0, 1) independently over c,    yc = 1{ εc ≤ ηict }  ⟹  P(yc = 1) = 1 − exp( −eηict )
(10)

This is the complementary log-log link. §10 shows it is also what a latent Poisson category count gives, and that the two routes agree — which is why the link and κ's scale are chosen together rather than separately.

Independence across categories is an assumption, not a derivation, and it is the one the generated data most visibly violates: 95% of within-basket product pairs are cross-category, and those pairs are produced by C independent coin flips.

7Breadth

Given entry, the number of additional distinct products beyond the first is Poisson, truncated so that kc cannot exceed what the store stocks:

P(kc = r ∣ yc = 1) = e−Λbrd (Λbrd)r−1 / (r−1)! ∑r′=1Nc e−Λbrd (Λbrd)r′−1 / (r′−1)!,    r = 1, …, Nc
(11)

The denominator is computed exactly as written, by log-sum-exp over r′ = 1…Nc, and is verified to sum to 1 at three (Λ, Nc) settings by eval/33_verify_equations.py.

Imposed, and it carries a store index the systematic term does not

Nothing in the utility structure implies a Poisson count; this is a modelling choice (§14). The truncation is not cosmetic: the denominator depends on Nc, which varies by store, while Λbrdic has no store index. Eq. 11 is therefore store-dependent through a channel Eq. 8 does not model. Dropping the denominator is not an option — the density would not sum to one on its stated support, and the process could emit kc > Nc, for which 𝒮(𝒦) is empty and Eq. 13 is 0/0.

8Which products

Given the composition 𝒦, the product set is drawn from a Gibbs measure on 𝒮(𝒦) — that is, a distribution of the form P(S) ∝ exp E(S) for a real-valued function E called the energy, which here is Eq. 12. This is the definition of the factor, not a consequence of anything; §10 derives its conditionals and proves no other distribution has them.

E(S) = ∑j ∈ S bijt + 1n − 1 ∑{j,k} ⊂ S αj⊤αk   (n ≥ 2),      E(S) = bijt  (n = 1)
(12)
P(S ∣ 𝒦) = exp E(S) ∑S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦) exp E(S′),    S ∈ 𝒮(𝒦)
(13)

The n = 1 case is stated separately rather than leaving 1/(n−1) multiplying an empty sum. 𝒮(𝒦) is finite and non-empty whenever kc ≤ Nc for every c, which Eq. 11 guarantees, so Eq. 13 is a well-defined distribution. Unlike the other three factors it does not decompose over products — that is its purpose.

Neither E(S) nor its normaliser is ever evaluated. §12 explains why they do not need to be, and §9 is what makes that possible.

9Units

P(qj = ν ∣ S) = e−Λqtyij (Λqtyij)ν−1 / (ν−1)!,    ν = 1, 2, …
(14)

Independent across j ∈ S, and imposed rather than derived (§14). Note the support is unbounded here while the data is censored at 12 (§3): the likelihood is that of an untruncated shifted Poisson evaluated on censored observations, which is a misspecification of known direction — it inflates Λqty — and unmeasured size.

Part IVWhy Eq. 13 is usable

10Swap connectedness, the swap identity, uniqueness

Lemma 1 — swap connectedness

Call S′ a swap of S if S′ = (S \ {j}) ∪ {m} for some j ∈ S and m ∈ 𝒞s(c(j)) \ S. Then every swap of S ∈ 𝒮(𝒦) lies in 𝒮(𝒦), and any two members of 𝒮(𝒦) are joined by a finite chain of swaps.

Proof. A swap replaces one product of category c(j) by another of the same category, so every count |S ∩ 𝒞s(c)| is unchanged and S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦). For connectedness take S, T ∈ 𝒮(𝒦). Categories partition the products, so treat each c separately: S and T each hold exactly kc products of 𝒞s(c). The sets S ∩ 𝒞s(c) \ T and T ∩ 𝒞s(c) \ S have equal size; swap them one pair at a time, each step staying inside 𝒞s(c). Doing this for every category reaches T in ∑c|S ∩ 𝒞s(c) \ T| swaps. ∎

Now take S ∈ 𝒮(𝒦), j ∈ S, m ∈ 𝒞s(c(j)) \ S and S′ = (S \ {j}) ∪ {m}. By Lemma 1, S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦), and |S′| = n so the factor 1/(n−1) is identical in E(S) and E(S′). Every b except bj and bm cancels, as does every pair involving neither. What survives is

E(S′) − E(S) = ( bimt − bijt ) + ( αm − αj )⊤ ᾱ−j = u−jm − u−jj
(15)

The step to u holds because m's context in S′ and j's context in S are the same vector: both equal (n−1)−1∑k∈S\{j}αk. Exponentiating and normalising over A−j gives the full conditional of Eq. 13 at one position:

P( j ∣ S \ {j}, 𝒦 ) = exp u−jj ∑m ∈ A−j exp u−jm
(16)

Lemma 2 — uniqueness

Eq. 13 is the only distribution on 𝒮(𝒦) whose single-position conditionals are Eq. 16.

Proof. Let Q be any such distribution. Eq. 16 gives positive probability to every admissible product, so Q(S) > 0 for every S reachable from a point of its support, and by Lemma 1 that is all of 𝒮(𝒦). For a swap pair (S, S′), the conditional at the position in question gives Q(S′)/Q(S) = exp(u−jm − u−jj) = exp(E(S′) − E(S)) by Eq. 15. Any T ∈ 𝒮(𝒦) is reached from a fixed S0 by a chain of swaps; telescoping along the chain gives Q(T)/Q(S0) = exp(E(T) − E(S0)), which depends only on the endpoints and so is independent of the chain. Summing to one fixes Q(S0). Hence Q is Eq. 13. ∎

Which theorem this is, and which it is not

This is Brook's telescoping argument, and it needs only swap-connectedness from Lemma 1. It does not use Besag's positivity condition, which requires the support to be a product of coordinate supports — false here, since 𝒮(𝒦) forbids repeats and fixes category counts. Hammersley–Clifford is a factorisation theorem and is not used anywhere in this document. An earlier draft of this specification invoked it; that was wrong, for exactly the reason just given.

10.1What symmetry is needed for, and what tying costs

Eq. 15 requires the pairwise interaction Wjk to be symmetric — otherwise no potential function exists, the conditionals of Eq. 16 are mutually incompatible, and P(S ∣ 𝒦) does not exist at all. It does not require W to be a Gram matrix: Wjk = ½(αj⊤ρk + ρj⊤αk) is symmetric for a free second embedding ρ and admits a potential. Tying ρ = α is therefore stronger than the theory needs.

What the tied form gives up

With W = AA⊤, W is positive semidefinite of rank at most K, so the model cannot represent an arbitrary symmetric co-purchase structure. Concretely ∑{j,k}⊂Sαj⊤αk = ½(‖∑j∈Sαj‖² − ∑j∈S‖αj‖²) ≥ −½∑j∈S‖αj‖², so the total repulsion the energy can express is bounded below: two products can be mildly substitutable but not arbitrarily so. Tying also forces one α to carry household taste in Eq. 4 and co-purchase in Eq. 12 simultaneously, which is an identification constraint (§15), not a free choice.

What is not claimed: that Eq. 13 has no random-utility representation. Any strictly positive distribution on a finite set is the argmax of {score + iid Gumbel} indexed by the elements of that set, so Eq. 13 is the argmax of E(S′) + εS′ over S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦). What fails is a representation with errors attached to positions and drawn once — which is exactly why §12 must redraw.

10.2The scale of κ

Eq. 7 contains κc without saying what value is neutral. One auxiliary construction fixes it. Suppose total units from category c were Q ~ Poisson(eaic + κcIV), split across the category in proportion to eb. Poisson thinning gives independent per-product counts with rate ea+κIV·eb−IV, since product j's share is eb−IV by Eq. 6, so

Qij ~ Poisson( eaic + (κc−1) IVict + bijt )
(17)

At κc = 1 the inclusive value cancels. And yc = 1{Q ≥ 1} gives P = 1 − e−rate, the same cloglog as Eq. 10, so the two routes to §6 agree. κ is initialised at exactly 1.0 so the data must move it off neutral.

The interpretation does not transfer wholesale

Eq. 17 is a different model from Eqs. 9–14: there one Poisson governs entry, breadth and units together, whereas here they are three separate factors and only entry sees IV. Therefore:

  • Valid. κc = 1 is the point at which a uniform rise in a category's product values raises its entry log-hazard by exactly that amount. κ < 1 damps the transmission, κ > 1 amplifies it.
  • Not valid. "At κ = 1 the category grows exactly as much as its products gained." Category volume also moves through breadth and units, neither of which sees IV at any κ.
  • Not valid. "κ = 0 means pure cannibalisation." At κ = 0 entry ignores IV, but a price cut still moves breadth through cpc and units through γq⊤βq. And κ = softplus(·) > 0 strictly, so κ = 0 is a limit the parameterisation cannot reach.

Eq. 17 is never evaluated. It fixes one number — the neutral point — and nothing else.

Part VEstimation, exactly as implemented

11The four components

Index trips by v. Write 𝒮v for the set of categories sampled for trip v (§11.2), M = 192 for the number of trips in a batch, and ℛv = ∑j∈Sv1 for its purchase rows. The four components, each written as the code computes it:

ℒitem = − 1∑vℛv ∑v ∑j ∈ Sv log P( j ∣ Sv \ {j}, 𝒦v )      ℒqty = − 1∑vℛv ∑v ∑j ∈ Sv log P(qj)

Eq. 21 at w = 1 is not the log-likelihood, and the gap is 24.5×

Each component above is a mean over its own terms. The joint log-likelihood of Eq. 9 is a sum, in which ℒinc carries M·C ≈ 36,096 summands against ℒitem's ≈ 1,476. Relative to the likelihood, the implemented objective therefore weights incidence about 24.5× lower at wc = 1; ℒqty is exact, since it shares ℒitem's terms, and ℒbrd is off by about 0.83.

Both were fitted. Setting wc = 24.46 to recover the likelihood weighting costs 63% of the price coefficient (+0.695 → +0.260, roughly 42 refit standard deviations) for 0.0035 nats of incidence likelihood, and item likelihood gets slightly worse too. The mechanism is mechanical: γ and β enter b, b enters IV, IV enters incidence, so up-weighting entry drags the shared price parameters toward what category entry wants while the item head — which carries 79% of the own-price elasticity — is down-weighted.

Neither weighting is correct a priori, because §13 shows any w ≠ 1 forfeits the pseudo-likelihood consistency argument and wc = 24.46 forfeits it no less than wc = 1. Every fitted result in this project uses the mean-over-terms convention. It is recorded here rather than resolved.

11.1The item component is exact, and there is no negative sampling

ℒitem is Besag's pseudo-likelihood for Eq. 13 — the sum of one-position conditionals. Each conditional is Eq. 16, a softmax over A−j, which has a median of 15 and at most 225 members. That sum is affordable exactly, so it is computed exactly: no negatives are sampled anywhere in this model. Consequences worth stating, because earlier versions of this work did sample:

  • No proposal distribution, hence no log q correction and no metric whose value depends on how decoys were drawn.
  • Item log-likelihoods reported here are not comparable to any figure computed against sampled decoys, whose optimal scorer is log p − log q rather than log p. Only distance from chance carries across.
  • Chance on this conditional is −log 46.9 = −3.848, where 46.9 is the mean size of A−j on held-out data.

Implementation detail that is part of the model: A−j is built as the category block, masked by (i) whether the store carries the product, (ii) the category-block padding, and (iii) exclusion of the basket's other products of that same category. The purchased product is then forced admissible. Item (iii) is Eq. 3's set constraint; without it a category could be given the same product twice.

11.2Category sampling

Eq. 10 is a Bernoulli over 188 categories per trip, of which 6.12 are entered on average. Sixteen categories are sampled uniformly and without replacement, and ℒinc averages over them.

Case-control sampling — deliberately drawing entered and non-entered categories in fixed proportion — is rejected for a reason specific to the link. The standard log(π1/π0) intercept correction is a logistic result (Prentice–Pyke): only for the logit does outcome-dependent sampling shift the intercept alone and leave slopes intact. Eq. 10 is cloglog, for which no such invariance holds, so outcome-dependent sampling would distort the slopes including κ and could not be repaired by an offset. Uniform sampling avoids the question entirely.

11.3The inclusive value is estimated in training and exact in generation

Eq. 6 is a log-sum-exp over up to 225 products. In training, m = 32 of the Nc stocked products are sampled uniformly with replacement and the sum is rescaled:

ÎVict = log( ∑r=1m ebijrt ) + log Ncm,    m = 32
(22)

The sum inside is unbiased, since 𝔼[(Nc/m)∑reb] = ∑jeb; its logarithm is biased low by Jensen's inequality. Both facts are checked numerically by eval/33_verify_equations.py.

Training and generation do not use the same inclusive value

Training uses Eq. 22. Generation uses Eq. 6 exactly, summing over the full stocked block with no sampling and no rescaling (28_nested_counterfactual.py, cat_utilities). The frozen reference IVc is built with the sampled estimator, so it carries the sampling bias while the quantity it is subtracted from, at generation time, does not.

Measured on 394 (trip, category) cells: the Jensen bias of Eq. 22 is −0.0431 at m = 32. Generation's centred term is therefore about 0.043 higher than training's on average, which at κ ≈ 0.87 is +0.037 on η and roughly a 3.8% higher entry rate. This is a genuine train/generate mismatch and it is not in the model's favour or against it in any simple way — generated baskets contain 12% too few categories, so correcting this alone would widen that gap.

The measured effect of the estimator on κ, which is not what theory predicted

Write e := ÎVict − IVict for the estimator's error, used only in this box. Classical errors-in-variables predicts κ̂ is attenuated toward zero by Var(IV)/(Var(IV)+Var(e)). Measured directly: within-category Var(IV) = 0.293 and Var(e) = 0.0945 / 0.0475 / 0.0224 at m = 32 / 64 / 128, halving with m exactly as expected, implying attenuation 0.756 / 0.861 / 0.929 and predicting κ̂ ≈ 0.99 at m = 64 and ≈ 1.07 at m = 128, from 0.872 at m = 32.

Refitting gives 0.872 → 0.869 → 0.828. The prediction fails with the wrong sign. The classical result does not transfer because e is not classical error: it is correlated with the true IV, since the sampling error of a log-sum-exp depends on how concentrated that category's b values are, and it is biased rather than mean-zero.

Two consequences. The sign conclusion is robust — κ < 1 with 0.0% of categories above 1 at every m tested. The precision is not: κ's spread across seeds is 0.0011, while changing this nuisance constant moves it 0.045, forty times as much. Any interval on κ built from seed variation understates its uncertainty by that factor, and per-category κ correlates only +0.73 between m = 32 and m = 128.

11.4The frozen reference

IVc is fixed at iteration 500 to the mean of ÎVict over a uniform sample of categories, and never updated thereafter. It must come from a uniform sample: taken from the training batches it would over-represent categories the trip bought, whose IV is higher, and the frozen reference would then sit above a typical category's IV. Being a fixed per-category constant it is absorbed by c0c and does not change the fit — freezing exists only to guarantee training and generation subtract the same number, which §11.3 shows is achieved for the reference but not for the IV itself.

12Optimisation and selection

Adam at learning rate 0.005 annealed by cosine to 0.05× over 6,000 iterations; batches of 192 trips expanded to all their purchase rows. Validation runs every 1,000 iterations and the checkpoint is kept on ℒitem − wqℒqty − wcℒinc − wbℒbrd, evaluated on held-out data. Because Eq. 16 is exact, the item term of that criterion is a true held-out conditional likelihood and needs no surrogate or calibration.

13What consistency does and does not hold

The textbook pseudo-likelihood guarantee does not apply here

Two obstructions, both structural.

Reweighting. Besag's consistency is for the maximiser of ℒitem alone. Eq. 21 maximises a weighted sum of four log-likelihoods, and unless wq = wc = wb = 1 and the components share no parameters, its maximiser is not an M-estimator for the parameters of any one of them — an M-estimator being one defined as the maximiser of a sample average whose population counterpart is maximised at the true parameter, which is the property that delivers consistency. They do share parameters — α and γ, β appear in all four — so the weights are not innocuous even at 1. §11 shows they are not at 1 relative to the likelihood in any case.

Incidental parameters. θi, γi, cui, hi and γqi are per-household: 2,066 households with roughly 145 parameters each and a bounded number of trips per household. Classical consistency requires the parameter count to stay fixed as the sample grows. This is the Neyman–Scott regime, in which the common parameters are generally inconsistent as well.

What can honestly be claimed: Eq. 21 is a penalised M-estimator whose population objective is maximised at the truth when the weights match the likelihood and the penalties vanish. Consistency of κ or of γ⊤β needs an argument that the household parameters are ancillary, or a fixed-effects treatment, or an explicit prior. None is supplied, here or anywhere in this project.

14The sampler

The normaliser of Eq. 13 is a sum over

|𝒮(𝒦)| = ∏(c,kc) ∈ 𝒦 Nc! kc! (Nc − kc)!
(23)

measured on 3,000 held-out compositions at median 106.7, 90th percentile 1028.6, maximum 10124. Neither E(S) nor this normaliser is ever evaluated; Eq. 15 makes that unnecessary, because only ratios between baskets differing in one position are needed and those are Eq. 16, a sum over at most 225 terms.

A trip is generated in three stages:

  1. Composition. Compute ηict for all C categories using the exact IV of Eq. 6, draw yc from Eq. 10 independently, then draw kc = 1 + Poisson(Λbrd) for each entered category and cap it at the number of products the store stocks.
  2. Placement. For each (c, kc), draw kc distinct products from 𝒞s(c) without replacement, with probability proportional to eb. The context is zero here because no basket exists yet.
  3. Gibbs refinement. Visit the n positions in order for T sweeps. At position −j, rebuild A−j and ᾱ−j from the current draft and redraw from Eq. 16.

Every stage-3 move resamples one position from its exact conditional under Eq. 13, so Eq. 13 is invariant: a draft already distributed as Eq. 13 stays so. Lemma 1 gives irreducibility — every member of 𝒮(𝒦) is reachable from every other — and since j ∈ A−j the chain can stay put, so it is aperiodic, meaning it does not cycle deterministically. Those three properties together give convergence to Eq. 13 from any starting basket. One sweep costs n conditionals — measured at 8.44 positions × 42.5 admissible products ≈ 359 evaluations of Eq. 4.

Unresolved: T is free and no mixing diagnostic exists

Convergence is asymptotic in T. Nothing here bounds the error at finite T, the support sizes above make a slow case plausible, and no autocorrelation or coupling check has been run. T = 4 is used throughout. This should be settled before generated baskets are treated as draws from Eq. 13 rather than as the output of a fixed procedure.

Part VIIAssumptions and identification

16Assumptions the model needs to be correct

Grouped by what fails if the assumption fails.

Needed for Eq. 13 to exist at all

  1. The pairwise interaction is symmetric. §10.1. Violated by an untied ρ, under which the conditionals of Eq. 16 admit no joint distribution. The code ties ρ = α by default; the --untie-rho flag exists and, if used, Eq. 13 no longer describes what is fitted.
  2. kc ≤ Nc for every entered category. Guaranteed by the truncation in Eq. 11. Without it 𝒮(𝒦) is empty and Eq. 13 is 0/0.

Needed for the stated error structure

  1. Gumbel shocks are redrawn independently at every position and every visit. A single draw shared across positions would correlate the conditionals and Eq. 16 would not be their law. This is why §14 must redraw rather than sort once.
  2. Category shocks are independent across the C categories. Imposed. This is what makes which-categories a product of C coin flips, and 95% of within-basket product pairs are cross-category.
  3. Independence of irrelevant alternatives within a position. Two near-identical yoghurts draw independent shocks and behave as genuinely distinct options. The model does not relax this within a category; κ relaxes it across categories, which is the entire purpose of the nesting term.

Imposed with no derivation

  1. Breadth is shifted Poisson (Eq. 11) and units are shifted Poisson (Eq. 14). The count is the primitive; there is no latent utility beneath either. Both impose equidispersion, and basket counts are typically overdispersed; untested.
  2. Units are independent across products given S. All modelled dependence is in which products appear, none in how many.
  3. A hard floor on the modal line. q = 1 + Poisson gives P(q = 1) = e−Λ, which cannot fall below that for any Λ. A zero-truncated Poisson or a negative binomial would relax this at the cost of one parameter.
  4. Eqs. 11 and 14 are not Eq. 17. If a latent per-product count were Poisson, kc = #{j : Qij ≥ 1} would be Poisson-binomial, not truncated shifted Poisson, and qj would be zero-truncated Poisson, not 1 + Poisson. They share a functional form with Eq. 17, not a derivation.

Needed for the estimates to mean anything

  1. The household parameters are ancillary, or a fixed-effects or Bayesian treatment is supplied. None is (§13). Without one, neither κ nor γ⊤β is a consistent estimate of anything.
  2. The assortment 𝒞s(c) is what the shopper could have bought. It is defined as "sold at least once in training" (Appendix B.2), which is selection on the outcome. Measured consequence: the allocation elasticity runs from −0.57 to 0.00 as the inclusion threshold tightens from 1 to 25 sales. Part of that is mechanical, since shrinking the set drives πj → 1 and the elasticity carries a factor (1 − πj), and this test does not separate the two.
  3. Δlog p is the price the shopper faced. Only 24.7% of the product × day grid is observed; the rest is carried forward (Appendix B.1). Measured: within the same products, the price–demand association is −0.7383 in carried-forward weeks against −0.5424 in observed weeks, so roughly 27% of it sits in imputed prices.

17Identification

Four exact invariances, and what each implies for what may be reported.

  1. Rotation of α and θ. b contains θi⊤αj and E contains αj⊤αk; both are invariant under α → Rα, θ → Rθ for any orthogonal R. With K = 64 that is a K(K−1)/2 = 2,016-dimensional indeterminacy. Only inner products are identified, so any reported embedding geometry must be rotation-invariant. Cosine similarity and nearest-neighbour purity are; individual coordinates are not, and none is reported.
  2. Scale of α. Under α → cα, θ → θ/c the taste term is invariant but the interaction scales by c². The scale of α is therefore pinned — by the interaction, not by the taste term. This is the identifying role of tying ρ = α, and it disappears if ρ is untied.
  3. Sign and rotation of the price interaction. γi⊤βj is invariant under orthogonal rotation of Kp-space and under (γ, β) → (−γ, −β). Only the product is identified and nothing constrains it to be positive, so upward-sloping demand is representable. Measured: 24.02% of (household, product) pairs carry the economically wrong sign. Aggregate elasticities remain negative; no individual pair's elasticity should be quoted.
  4. Category levels. This is the consequential one. A−j ⊆ 𝒞s(c(j)), so ℒitem only ever compares products within a category: adding a constant to bijt for every j in one category leaves every Eq. 16 unchanged. The category level of b is therefore identified only through IV in Eq. 7 — that is, through the noisy, Jensen-biased Eq. 22, whose bias is absorbed by c0c, which is the same parameter the category level lives in.

Invariance 4 is not hypothetical, and it has been demonstrated twice

Once. Dropping (store, category) pairs with nothing stocked from ℒinc — 9.46% of sampled pairs, none of which is ever positive — fits c0 to a base rate 9.5% higher. Refitting with the rule obeyed moves held-out item likelihood by 0.0027, the price coefficient by 0.003, κ by 0.000, and generated categories per basket from 5.60 to 5.62. Nothing moves: the frozen reference falls from 0.264 to 0.057 and absorbs the entire correction.

Twice. Reducing the IV estimator's variance fourfold (§11.3) moves κ by −0.045 rather than the +0.28 attenuation theory predicts. If κ were identified by the IV variation this section says identifies it, a fourfold noise reduction would move it in the predicted direction.

Together these say the c0/reference pair absorbs changes that theory says should land on κ. What actually pins κ is therefore not established, and that is a stronger statement than the earlier draft's "identification is asserted, not tested".

18Inference

The estimands are the price elasticity (Appendix C) and κ. Neither is a maximum likelihood estimate, so an inverse Hessian is not a valid variance.

  • Sandwich. For a pseudo-likelihood the asymptotic variance is H−1VH−1, with H the expected Hessian and V the variance of the score. Because ℒitem sums n dependent conditionals per basket, V must be estimated with the basket as the independent unit, not the position.
  • Clustering. Trips repeat within households and the household parameters are estimated from those same trips, so the resampling unit is the household.
  • What is actually reported. Two things, and neither is the above. A household block bootstrap over the evaluation, which covers evaluation sampling only; and the spread over seven refits differing only in seed, which covers initialisation and optimisation. A bootstrap over the training sample, refitting each replicate, has never been run.

Seed spread is not a measure of how well a parameter is pinned

κ's spread across seven seeds is 0.0011. Changing m in Eq. 22 — a nuisance constant that no seed varies and that no theory selects — moves it 0.045. Reporting the former as κ's uncertainty understates it by a factor of forty. The same caution applies to every other parameter for which only seed spread is quoted.

BHow the conditioning data is built

B.1Δlog p

Price. The loyalty price a card holder faces: (SALES_VALUE − COUPON_MATCH_DISC) / QUANTITY. The coupon match is subtracted because it is a manufacturer refund, not a price the shopper responded to. The paid price is not used: two shoppers at one shelf with different coupons would otherwise face different prices for the same product on the same day.

Per product-day. The median over that day's purchase lines — median, not mean, because one mis-keyed quantity produces a wild per-unit value. 24.7% of the J × D grid is observed; missing days carry the last observed price forward, and days before a product's first sale carry the first observed price backward.

Centring. With 𝒯 the training days, Δlog pjt = log pjt − |𝒯|−1∑t′∈𝒯log pjt′, where log p = log max(price, 10−3).

Store deviation. Δsjsw = log p̃jsw − log p̃jw, the store's median against the chain median that week, kept where |Δs| > 10−6 — 244,880 cells, 0.53% of the grid, zero elsewhere. The model reads Δlog pjst = Δlog pjt + Δsjsw. The superscript distinguishes it from Djsw, the display indicator of B.4.

Two properties of this covariate, both measured

Three quarters of it is imputed, and that is where the association lives. Within the same products, the price–demand elasticity is −0.7383 in carried-forward weeks against −0.5424 in observed weeks (differential +0.1959, t = +35.3, over 3,869 products supplying both). Roughly 27% of the fitted association sits in prices that were never observed.

The centring looks ahead within training. The mean runs over all of 𝒯, including days after t, as does gσ(j) in B.3. This is not test leakage — 𝒯 excludes held-out days — but a training-day covariate does use information from later training days, which matters if the series is ever used sequentially.

B.2𝒞s(c)

Product j ∈ 𝒞s(c(j)) if store s sold it at least once in the training window. This covers 50.9% of the product × store grid.

The choice set is constructed from the choices

Defining assortment by "was sold" is selection on the outcome, not merely a stale proxy. A product stocked but never bought is deleted from the denominator of Eq. 16 and from the sum in Eq. 6. Both effects run one way: fitted choice probabilities are inflated and IV is inflated, and IV multiplied by κ is an estimand. An assortment measure independent of purchases does not exist in this dataset, so this cannot be resolved here — only bounded, which §16.11 does.

B.3Recency

With Δt the days since household i last bought a product of sub-commodity σ(j), by a strictly-before lookup — τ is not reused here, it is reserved for the penalty coefficients of Eq. 21:

xijt = [ 1{never before},   e−Δt/7,   e−Δt/gσ(j),   log(1+Δt)/log 100 ]⊤
(24)

with the last three set to zero when there is no earlier purchase. gσ(j) is that sub-commodity's median repurchase gap over 𝒯, so "about due" means a week for milk and months for shampoo. xict is the same four functions of the days since the household last bought anything in category c.

B.4Promotional placement

From dunnhumby's causal_data.csv, 36.8M rows giving display and mailer codes per PRODUCT_ID × STORE_ID × WEEK_NO. Every one of the 6,522,942 rows on our products has display ≠ 0 or mailer ≠ 0 — measured, not assumed — so the file records only promoted cells and absence is a genuine zero. D and M are therefore dense binary indicators, 1 where the file records any display or any mailer code respectively.

Placement is strongly related to price: promoted cells sit 0.0437 log points below unpromoted (t = −98), driven almost entirely by the mailer (−0.095 alone, −0.130 with display) rather than by display (−0.002). Conditioning on it reduces the fitted price coefficient from +0.695 to +0.475 while improving held-out fit. That makes it a control for an omitted cause of price, not an instrument: appearing in the weekly circular is advertising, which moves demand directly, so it fails the exclusion restriction — the requirement that an instrument affect the outcome only through the variable it instruments for.

CPrice derivatives

Four margins, one per factor of Eq. 9. Two distinct shares appear and they are not equal:

πIVj = ebijt ∑j′ ∈ 𝒞s(c) ebij′t      π−jj = eu−jj ∑m ∈ A−j eu−jm
(25)

πIV normalises b over the whole category and is what IV differentiates; π−j normalises u over the admissible set and is the model's actual choice probability. Writing gj = −(γi⊤βj) for ∂b/∂log pj:

∂ log π−jj∂ log pj = gj( 1 − π−jj )      ∂ log P(yc=1)∂ log pj = G(ηict) · κc gj πIVj
(26)
∂ log 𝔼[kc]∂ log pj ≈ − cpcNc Λbrd1 + Λbrd      ∂ log 𝔼[qj]∂ log pj = − (γq⊤iβqj) Λqty1 + Λqty
(27)

where G(η) = eηe−eη / (1 − e−eη) is the cloglog chain factor, the derivative of log P(y=1) with respect to η. G → 1 as η → −∞ and equals 0.983 at the observed base rate of 0.0325. The breadth derivative is approximate only in ignoring the truncation, which binds rarely. The first three are verified against the fitted model by autograd in eval/33_verify_equations.py; the breadth margin is never evaluated anywhere.

The margins do not collapse into one expression

Combining the first two into −(γ⊤β)[1 − π(1 − κc)] requires both G(η) = 1 and πIV = π−j. Neither is exact: G = 0.983 at the base rate, and the two shares normalise different scores over different sets. Report the margins separately, or state both approximations with their magnitudes.
