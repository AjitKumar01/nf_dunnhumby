Experiments

What the fitted model does, and does not, deliver

Every number here comes from one model, ps_nested, fitted to the specification in model_spec.html. That sentence is the point of this rewrite: the previous version of this page mixed results from two different models and from a measurement it retracted in one section and relied on in another.

1Protocol, and what is comparable

All numbers are on held-out test weeks. The item score is the exact conditional of Eq. 15 of the specification — a softmax over the purchased product's category minus whatever else the basket holds from it — so it depends on no sampling scheme. Its choice set averages 46.9 admissible products, making chance −log 46.9 = −3.848 and 2.1% top-1.

What changed since the previous version of this page

Sections on generation, representations, price sensitivity, targeting and limitations were computed on spec_nested, a superseded fit, while the uncertainty, baseline and placebo sections used ps_nested. The page named no model anywhere, so the mixture was invisible. Every section has been recomputed on ps_nested. Two conclusions changed sign as a result, and both are marked where they occur (§9 and §12).

Separately, the old §11 temperature table was produced by a version of scripts/eval/42_limitations.py that attributed each generated basket to the wrong household — the same bug the old §10 explicitly retracted, two sections earlier. It was fixed in commit 95cc0fd, seventeen hours after that artifact was written, and the page was never regenerated.

Item log-likelihoods from before the category rewrite are not comparable to anything here: they scored a 21-way or 201-way discrimination against sampled decoys, whose optimal scorer is log p − log q rather than log p. Only distance from chance carries across.

2Does the code compute the specification?

Thirty checks compare each printed formula against the fitted model — by autograd where the specification states a derivative, by direct evaluation where it states an identity, and by rebuilding the quantity from the raw training split where it states a definition (scripts/eval/33_verify_equations.py). All thirty pass, on all four models fitted for this page.

They did not pass before, and nothing said so

This page previously claimed "fifteen checks, all pass". At the time of writing that sentence the suite could not run at all: main() took its label as a default argument and nothing read argv, so the documented --label silently loaded a pre-specification checkpoint that no longer loads. Behind that, the quantity check still read candidate slot 0 after the loss was fixed to index the purchased product, so it had been failing since 8 August. The term-by-term check for Eq. 4 omitted two of its terms and passed anyway, because the hard-coded pair it tested happened to have zero purchase history.

Two further claims — that the cloglog link was "verified to 3.3e−06" and the truncated breadth "sums to 1" — had no corresponding check anywhere in the repository. Both now do.

The specification's objective buys 0.0035 nats of incidence likelihood and pays 63% of the price coefficient — a fall of about 42 refit standard deviations (§3) — while item likelihood also gets slightly worse. The mechanism is mechanical: γ and β enter b, b enters IV, and IV enters incidence, so up-weighting entry drags the shared price parameters toward what category entry wants, while the item head, which carries 79% of the own-price elasticity (§6), is correspondingly down-weighted. Neither weighting is a priori correct — §16 of the specification forfeits pseudo-likelihood consistency at any w ≠ 1 — but the implemented convention is what every result here was produced under, and it preserves the estimand.

What neither interval covers

Variability from resampling the training households. That needs a refit per replicate at roughly 25 minutes each and has not been run. The seed replicates are a partial substitute: they hold the training sample fixed and vary only the optimisation.

Persistence and the basket interaction follow at 0.071 and 0.070, then the placebo at 0.059, then state and nest.

The ordering changed, and the reason is informative

On the superseded fit the household recency state was worth +0.049 and the basket interaction +0.058. Here state has halved to +0.026 while the interaction has grown to +0.070. The persistence terms are what did it: ωij and fij are per-(household, product) purchase history, which overlaps heavily with what xijt was carrying. Adding persistence did not simply add signal — it absorbed roughly half of the recency state's job. Two terms that look independent in the specification are substitutes in the fit.

κ is stable at 0.865–0.872 across every variant including the placebo, and sits below 1 throughout: a price cut reaches category entry at less than full strength regardless of what else is removed. That matters because κ is the interpretive centrepiece and its refit sd is 0.0011, so this range is wide relative to noise but narrow in substance.

The negative control does what a negative control should

The quantity head never enters item utility — it is a separate Poisson on units — so removing it must not move the item score if the metric is measuring what it claims. It moves it by −0.0005, inside refit noise and marginally in the wrong direction, which is exactly the signature of a null. This row is the only evidence here that the ablation procedure can return "no effect" at all, and it is why the other seven rows are worth reading.

Removing the nest lowers the price coefficient to +0.596 from +0.695 — the only ablation that moves it down materially. With no κ·IV channel, price can no longer reach category entry at all, and the fit compensates by shrinking the coefficient rather than redistributing it. Every other ablation nudges it up, toward +0.71–0.75, as the removed term's explanatory work is partly reassigned to price.

Not a fair fight, and it should not be read as one

Same metric and same choice sets, but not the same tuning budget: one tuned scalar per baseline against 881,490 parameters and an architecture selected over many runs. SHOPPER is also Bayesian and maximises an ELBO, while this fits the same model by MAP with an L2 penalty — the mode of the same posterior. So this compares models, not inference procedures, and the SHOPPER column carries no uncertainty of its own. Read it as a floor.

What this does and does not show

Permuting a product's price series across days destroys the price–demand association whether it is causal or confounded. The test rules out a spurious constant. It cannot distinguish "price moves demand" from "the retailer times promotions to demand", which is the actual endogeneity concern for within-product price variation. There is no instrument here and no discontinuity. This section is deliberately not titled "is the price response causal?" — the design does not answer that.

The price-recovery check is circular

It re-estimates a quantity the model was fitted to, so it verifies that the sampler propagates the coefficient into synthetic transactions — not that the coefficient is right. It is a plumbing check, not evidence about the elasticity.

This ordering is economically sensible, and it is stable

Cigarettes and baby food are the textbook inelastic goods — addiction and non-negotiable need. Bath tissue, cold cereal and bacon are storable, heavily promoted and brand-switchable, exactly where a shopper can respond. The model was given no category labels and no elasticity priors. The same ten categories appear in the same two groups on the superseded fit, with only minor reordering, so this is the most robust evidence here that γ⊤β estimates something real.

Two negative findings about gij

24.02% of (household, product) pairs have the wrong sign — the model says those households buy more when the product is dearer. Nothing in the specification constrains γ⊤β to be positive (§18), so upward-sloping demand is representable and the fit uses it for a quarter of all pairs. Aggregate elasticities remain negative, but no individual (household, product) elasticity should be quoted.

Household price sensitivity is unrelated to income: Spearman +0.0054, p = 0.891, n = 655. The expected economic pattern — poorer households more price sensitive — is entirely absent. Combined with §8, the household side of the price interaction has no demonstrated external validity.

The choice margin and the quantity margin are fitted separately and agree only weakly: Spearman +0.137 across products. Nothing imposes agreement, so this is a measurement rather than a check — but "price-sensitive product" means something different at the two margins.

11Can generated baskets train a Markovian policy?

A pricing policy learned on synthetic baskets needs the generator to behave as a Markov decision process. This model has one dynamic state — the purchase-recency vector xijt — so the question is whether generation reproduces its distribution. Matching each generated basket to its own household:

Drift, and this is a lower bound

The ratio grows 1.156 → 1.261 across twelve trips, so the per-basket excess accumulates rather than staying a level effect. And it is a lower bound: generation reads the recency state from the real purchase history, so a generated novel product does not make the next generated basket more novel. A true rollout would feed generated purchases back and compound further.

Three structural obstructions are untouched by any of this:

  • Prices are exogenous. The model conditions on Δlog p and never generates it, so there is no feedback from a policy's action to future prices.
  • Δlog p is centred on the training window. A policy that shifts the price level moves products outside the support the coefficients were fitted on.
  • No budget constraint. Nothing couples spending across categories, so a policy cutting prices everywhere produces unbounded purchasing rather than substitution.

Single-step counterfactuals of the kind in §6 are supported. Multi-step rollouts are usable over short horizons at most.

The estimand is not invariant to a threshold nobody has justified

The allocation elasticity runs from −0.57 to 0.00 as the threshold moves from 1 to 25 sales. Part of that is mechanical and should be said plainly: the elasticity is −(γ⊤β)(1 − πj), and shrinking the choice set drives πj → 1, so (1 − πj) → 0 by construction. This test therefore does not isolate outcome selection from that dependence.

But it establishes something arguably worse than the original concern: the headline elasticity depends on a modelling choice nobody has justified. "Sold at least once" was picked because a threshold of three deleted genuine purchases; no argument was ever given that it is the right definition of what a shopper could have bought. The sensitivity is first-order, not second.

Reversal — this page previously concluded the opposite

The old table reported 46.12% novelty at T = 1 falling only to 43.48% at T = 0.25, and concluded "the hypothesis is wrong; whatever produces the novelty is not the sharpness of the item softmax". Both halves of that were artefacts of the attribution bug. Measured correctly, novelty at T = 1 is 19.23% against a real 14.24%, and sharpening to T = 0.70 takes it past the real rate to 11.32%.

Sharpness is a live lever. What kills it as a fix is the confound the old section correctly identified: scaling the item utility also scales the inclusive value, which feeds incidence, so basket size inflates from 7.55 to 12.39 items on the way. A clean test would scale only inside the item softmax and leave IV alone. That has not been run.

Decomposing the change: the same corrected code on the superseded model gives 27.33% at T = 1, so of the 46.12 → 19.23 movement roughly 19 points are the bug and 8 are the model. The two causes were confounded in the original comparison and are separated here by refitting both.

A joint test

Every likelihood here is a conditional, and §7 compares summary statistics one at a time, which cannot see a joint mismatch. A classifier two-sample test can: train a discriminator to separate real baskets from generated ones on basket-level features. AUC 0.5 means indistinguishable.

13What is still not established

  1. Refitting uncertainty is estimated from seeds, not resampled data. Eight fits give the initialisation and optimisation component (sd 0.0040); variability from resampling the training households needs a refit per replicate and has not been run.
  2. The test set has been used repeatedly — across model comparisons, the specification rewrite, and this audit. Nothing reserves it for a single final evaluation, and the number of looks is now well into the dozens.
  3. The price response is not identified against confounding. The placebo rules out a spurious constant; it cannot separate price from promotion timing. dunnhumby ships causal_data.csv with display and mailer placement per product × store × week, which is read by archive/normalizing_flow/04_extras.py but never reaches basket_input/. Wiring it in is the highest-value open step.
  4. Identification is asserted, not tested — a 2,016-dimensional rotational invariance in α and θ, an unconstrained sign on γ⊤β now measured at 24.02% negative, and category levels identified only through the noisy inclusive value. §2's edge-case result is a live demonstration that these invariances absorb real changes.
  5. The assortment threshold is unjustified and the estimand is sensitive to it (§12). Resolving it needs an assortment measure independent of purchases, which this data does not contain.
  6. Generation is distinguishable at AUC 0.77, under-produces basket size by 9% and categories by 12% for reasons not established, under-produces fine substitution by 25%, and drifts over a horizon (1.156 → 1.261 over twelve trips, a lower bound).
  7. The IV estimator's attenuation is unmeasured. §17.1 of the specification notes κ̂ is biased toward zero by roughly Var(IV)/(Var(IV)+Var(e)) at m = 32 against Nc up to 225. Neither the attenuation factor nor its sensitivity to m has been computed, and κ is the interpretive centrepiece.
  8. SHOPPER is a reimplementation fitted by MAP, not the authors' variational CUDA code.
