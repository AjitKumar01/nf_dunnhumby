Specification

The nested basket model

Part I gives the random variables and the systematic terms that index them. Part II defines the distribution. Part III proves the three properties Part II relies on. Part IV is estimation, identification and inference. Assumptions that are imposed rather than derived are marked in amber wherever they occur.

Part IVariables and terms

1What is given

Everything is conditional on an observed trip descriptor:

𝒢 = ( i,   s,   t,   w,   {Δlog pjst}j=1..J )
(1)

household i (N = 2,066), store s (115), day t (D = 712), week-of-year w (52), one price deviation per product. Fixed index sets: J = 5,455 products, C = 188 categories, c(j) the category of product j, and 𝒞s(c) the products of category c stocked by store s (median 15, max 225). Construction of Δlog p and 𝒞s(c) is Appendix B.

3Systematic terms

Functions of 𝒢 and the parameters. Every symbol is used for exactly one thing; Appendix A is the full table.

3.1 Product appeal, no basket.

bijt = λj + θi⊤αj − (γi⊤βj) Δlog pjst + ψj⊤xijt + μj⊤δw + ζj⊤ξs + wloyjωij + wfrqjfij
(4)

Persistence. The last two terms are a rank-free per-(household, product) habit, and they are not decoration: θi⊤αj is a rank-K bilinear form standing in for an N × J table, so it can express "this household likes this kind of product" but not "this household buys this product and no other". With 𝒯 the training window,

n𝒯ij = #{ v ∈ 𝒯 : i(v) = i,   j ∈ Sv },     ωij = n𝒯ij max(1, ∑j′∈𝒞(c(j)) n𝒯ij′),     fij = log(1 + n𝒯ij)log 100
(4a)

ωij is the household's within-category share of product j — exactly the quantity Eq. 15 normalises — so ∑j∈𝒞(c)ωij = 1 for any category the household has ever bought from, and 0 otherwise. fij is the absolute strength of the habit, and fic = log(1 + ∑j′∈𝒞(c)n𝒯ij′) / log 100 is its category-level counterpart in Eq. 6. All three are computed on 𝒯 only, so they are past lookups rather than leakage, and all three are fixed data, not parameters; only the per-product loadings wloyj, wfrqj and the per-category chabc are estimated. Because they are frozen at the training window they do not update within a generated rollout, which is one reason §12's sampler is not a rollout.

3.2 Product appeal in a position. A position of a basket S is identified by its occupant: write −j for the position held by j, and A−j for its admissible products. For any candidate m:

A−j = 𝒞s(c(j)) \ (S \ {j}),     ᾱ−j = 1n−1 ∑k∈S, k≠j αk,     u−jm = bimt + αm⊤ᾱ−j
(5)

Superscript = position, subscript = candidate. Every candidate for a position is scored against the same ᾱ−j, since the other n−1 occupants are fixed. Note j ∈ A−j. For n = 1 set ᾱ−j = 0.

3.3 Category appeal.

IVict = log ∑j∈𝒞s(c) ebijt,     ηict = c0c + cu⊤iccc + cs⊤cxict + chabcfic + κc( IVict − IVc )
(6)

IV is built from b, not u: it is evaluated before any product exists. IVc is a fixed constant (§17.3). κc = softplus(κrawc) ∈ (0, ∞). fic is the category-level habit lookup of Eq. 4a. Note that IV sums eb over the category, and b now carries the Eq. 4a persistence terms, so a household's habits reach category entry through the inclusive value as well as through chab.

chab is in the model and earns almost nothing

Ablating it moves held-out item likelihood by about +0.006 nats and generated baskets not at all, which is inside refit noise. The incidence likelihood has roughly 0.012 nats of headroom to an oracle, so the objective cannot reward it. It is specified here because the fitted model contains it and it is active on 91% of categories — not because it is doing useful work. There is currently no flag to disable it, which is a defect: a term this weak should be optional.

3.4 Count rates.

Λbrdic = exp( cbc + hi − cpc Δlog pct ),    Λqtyij = exp( aj − (γq⊤iβqj) Δlog pjst + ψq⊤jxijt )
(7)

xijt, xict are four-vectors of purchase recency (Appendix B.3). Δlog pct is the mean of Δlog p over 𝒞s(c).

Part IIThe distribution

4The joint

P(ℬ | 𝒢, n ≥ 1) = ∏c P(yc) · ∏c:yc=1 P(kc) · P(S | 𝒦) · ∏j∈S P(qj) 1 − ∏c P(yc = 0)
(8)

A trip enters the data only because something was bought, so the model must condition on n ≥ 1. The unconditional product assigns ∏cP(yc=0) = exp(−∑ceηict) > 0 to the empty basket, which is never observed; the denominator removes it. Omitting this renormalisation misplaces mass into the intercepts c0c, which are the same parameters κ is estimated alongside.

5Category entry

Each category draws an independent shock from the minimum-form Gumbel, with CDF F(x) = 1 − exp(−ex), and is entered when the shock falls below its appeal:

εc ~ Gumbelmin(0,1),    yc = 1{ εc ≤ ηict }   ⟹   P(yc = 1) = 1 − exp(−eηict)
(9)

A complementary log-log link. §13 shows it is also what a latent Poisson category count gives, and that the two routes agree.

6Breadth

Let Nc = |𝒞s(c)|. The count of additional products beyond the first is Poisson, truncated so that kc cannot exceed the store's stock:

P(kc = r | yc=1) = e−Λbrdic (Λbrdic)r−1 / (r−1)! ∑r′=1Nc e−Λbrdic (Λbrdic)r′−1 / (r′−1)!,    r = 1, …, Nc
(10)

Imposed, not derived — and it carries a store index

Nothing in the utility structure implies a Poisson count; this is a modelling choice (§14). The truncation is not cosmetic: the denominator depends on Nc, which varies by store, while Λbrdic carries no store index. So Eq. 10 is store-dependent through a channel the systematic term does not model. Dropping the denominator instead leaves a density that does not sum to one on the stated support, and admits kc > Nc, for which 𝒮(𝒦) = ∅ and Eq. 12 is 0/0.

7Which products

Given 𝒦, the product set is drawn from a Gibbs measure on 𝒮(𝒦). This is the definition of the factor; §10 derives its conditionals and §11 shows no other distribution has them.

E(S) = ∑j∈S bijt + 1n−1 ∑{j,k}⊂S αj⊤αk   (n ≥ 2),     E(S) = bijt  (n = 1)
(11)
P(S | 𝒦) = exp E(S) ∑S′∈𝒮(𝒦) exp E(S′),    S ∈ 𝒮(𝒦)
(12)

Eq. 11 states the n = 1 case explicitly rather than leaving 1/(n−1) multiplying an empty sum. 𝒮(𝒦) is finite and non-empty whenever kc ≤ Nc for all c, which Eq. 10 guarantees, so Eq. 12 is a well-defined distribution. Unlike the other three factors it does not decompose over products.

8Units

P(qj = ν | S) = e−Λqtyij (Λqtyij)ν−1 / (ν−1)!,    ν = 1, 2, …
(13)

Independent across j ∈ S. Imposed, not derived (§14).

Part IIIProperties of Eq. 12

9Swap connectedness

Lemma 1

Call S′ a swap of S if S′ = (S \ {j}) ∪ {m} for some j ∈ S and m ∈ 𝒞s(c(j)) \ S. Every swap of S ∈ 𝒮(𝒦) is in 𝒮(𝒦), and any two members of 𝒮(𝒦) are joined by a finite chain of swaps.

Proof. A swap replaces one product of category c(j) by another of the same category, so every |S ∩ 𝒞s(c)| is unchanged and S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦). For connectedness take S, T ∈ 𝒮(𝒦). Categories partition the products, so consider each c separately: S and T each hold exactly kc products of 𝒞s(c). Enumerate S ∩ 𝒞s(c) \ T and T ∩ 𝒞s(c) \ S — equal-sized sets — and swap them one pair at a time, each step staying inside 𝒞s(c). Doing this for every c reaches T in ∑c|S∩𝒞s(c) \ T| swaps. ∎

10The swap identity and the conditional

Let S ∈ 𝒮(𝒦), j ∈ S, m ∈ 𝒞s(c(j)) \ S, and S′ = (S\{j}) ∪ {m}. By Lemma 1, S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦) and |S′| = n, so the factor 1/(n−1) is the same in E(S) and E(S′). Every b except bj, bm cancels, as does every pair involving neither. What remains:

E(S′) − E(S) = (bim − bij) + (αm − αj)⊤ᾱ−j = u−jm − u−jj
(14)

The step to u holds because m's context in S′ and j's context in S are the same vector ᾱ−j = (n−1)−1∑k∈S\{j}αk. Exponentiating and normalising over A−j gives the full conditional of Eq. 12 at one position:

P(j | S\{j}, 𝒦) = exp u−jj ∑m∈A−j exp u−jm
(15)

By McFadden's theorem Eq. 15 is exactly the law of argmaxm∈A−j[u−jm + εm] with εm iid Gumbel(0,1) over A−j. The shocks are redrawn independently at every position and every visit; a single draw shared across positions would correlate the conditionals and Eq. 15 would not be their law.

11Uniqueness

Lemma 2

Eq. 12 is the only distribution on 𝒮(𝒦) whose single-position conditionals are Eq. 15.

Proof. Let Q be any such distribution. Eq. 15 assigns positive probability to every admissible product, so Q(S) > 0 for every S reachable from a point of its support; by Lemma 1 that is all of 𝒮(𝒦). For a swap pair (S, S′) the conditional at the position in question gives

Q(S′)Q(S) = exp u−jm exp u−jj = exp( E(S′) − E(S) )
(16)

using Eq. 14. By Lemma 1 any T ∈ 𝒮(𝒦) is reached from a fixed S0 by a chain of swaps; telescoping Eq. 16 along the chain gives Q(T)/Q(S0) = exp(E(T) − E(S0)), independent of the chain taken since the right side depends only on the endpoints. Summing to one fixes Q(S0), so Q is Eq. 12. ∎

This is Brook's telescoping argument. It needs only swap-connectedness, from Lemma 1. It does not use Besag's positivity condition, which requires the support to be a product of coordinate supports — false here, since 𝒮(𝒦) forbids repeats and fixes category counts. Hammersley–Clifford is a factorisation theorem and is not used.

What symmetry is needed for, and what it is not needed for. Eq. 14 requires the pairwise interaction Wjk to be symmetric. It does not require W to be a Gram matrix: Wjk = ½(αj⊤ρk + ρj⊤αk) is symmetric for free ρ and admits a potential. Tying ρ = α is a stronger restriction than the theory needs, and it costs something:

Cost of the tied form

W = AA⊤ is positive semidefinite of rank ≤ K, so the model cannot represent an arbitrary symmetric co-purchase structure. Concretely ∑{j,k}⊂Sαj⊤αk = ½(‖∑j∈Sαj‖² − ∑j∈S‖αj‖²) ≥ −½∑j∈S‖αj‖², so the total substitution the energy can express is bounded below. Tying also forces the same α to carry household taste in Eq. 4 and co-purchase in Eq. 11, which is an identification constraint (§18), not a free choice.

What is not claimed: that Eq. 12 has no random-utility representation. Any strictly positive distribution on a finite set is the argmax of {score + iid Gumbel} indexed by the elements of that set, so Eq. 12 is the argmax of E(S′) + εS′ over S′ ∈ 𝒮(𝒦). What fails is a representation with errors attached to positions and drawn once: that is what forces the sampler in §12 to redraw.

12Sampling

The normaliser of Eq. 12 is a sum over

|𝒮(𝒦)| = ∏(c,kc)∈𝒦 Nc! kc!(Nc−kc)!
(17)

measured on 3,000 held-out compositions at median 106.7, 90th percentile 1028.6, maximum 10124. Neither E(S) nor the normaliser is evaluated. Only Eq. 15 is, a sum over at most 225 terms.

  1. For each (c, kc) ∈ 𝒦 draw kc distinct products from 𝒞s(c) in proportion to eb, without replacement.
  2. Visit the n positions in a random order. At position −j: form A−j and ᾱ−j from the current S, draw from Eq. 15, update S.
  3. Repeat step 2 for T sweeps and return S.

Each move is a Gibbs step for Eq. 12, so Eq. 12 is invariant. Lemma 1 gives irreducibility on 𝒮(𝒦); since j ∈ A−j the self-transition has positive probability, so the chain is aperiodic. Irreducible + aperiodic + invariant ⟹ convergence to Eq. 12 from any start. One sweep costs n conditionals, each a sum over |A−j| candidates — measured at 8.44 positions × 42.5 admissible products ≈ 359 evaluations of Eq. 5 per sweep.

Unresolved

T is a free parameter and no mixing diagnostic is reported. Convergence is asymptotic in T; nothing here bounds the error at finite T, and the support sizes above make a worst case plausible. This needs an autocorrelation or coupling check before generated baskets are used as evidence.

13What κ is, and what it is not

In the model as specified, κc appears in Eq. 6 and nowhere else. It scales how far the inclusive value moves the log-hazard of entering category c. Breadth (Eq. 7) and units (Eq. 7) contain no IV, and the product-choice factor (Eq. 12) contains no κ. Anything said about κ must be a statement about entry alone.

The scale is fixed by an auxiliary construction. Suppose the total units from category c were Q ~ Poisson(eaic+κcIV) split across the category in proportion to eb. Poisson thinning gives independent per-product counts with rates ea+κIV·eb−IV, since product j's share is eb−IV by Eq. 6, so

Qij ~ Poisson( eaic + (κc−1)IVict + bijt )
(18)

At κc = 1 the inclusive value cancels. And yc = 1{Q > 0} gives P = 1 − e−rate, the same cloglog as Eq. 9 — so the two routes to §5 agree, which is why the link and κ's scale are chosen together.

The interpretation does not transfer wholesale

Eq. 18 is a different model from Eqs. 8–13: there, one Poisson governs entry, breadth and units together; here they are three separate factors and only entry sees IV. So:

  • Valid: κc = 1 is the point at which a uniform rise in a category's product appeals raises its entry log-hazard by exactly the amount the appeals rose. κ < 1 damps that transmission, κ > 1 amplifies it.
  • Not valid: "at κ = 1 the category grows exactly as much as its products gained." Category volume also moves through breadth and units, neither of which sees IV at any κ.
  • Not valid: "κ = 0 means pure cannibalisation." At κ = 0 entry ignores IV, but a price cut still moves breadth via cpc and units via γq⊤βq. Moreover κc = softplus(·) > 0 strictly, so κ = 0 is a limit the parameterisation cannot attain.

Eq. 18 is never evaluated. It fixes one number — the neutral point — and κ is initialised at 1.0 so the data must move it off.

14What is imposed rather than derived

§5 and §10 follow from stated error distributions. Eqs. 10 and 13 do not: the count is the primitive, with no latent utility beneath it.

  • Equidispersion. A Poisson ties variance to mean; basket counts are typically overdispersed. Untested.
  • Units independent given S. Eq. 13 makes qj independent across products. All modelled dependence is in which products appear, none in how many.
  • A hard floor on the modal line. q = 1 + Poisson gives P(q = 1) = e−Λ, which cannot fall below that value for any Λ. A zero-truncated Poisson or negative binomial would relax this at the cost of one more parameter.
  • Not the same object as Eq. 18. If a latent per-product count were Poisson, kc = #{j : Qij ≥ 1} would be Poisson-binomial, not truncated shifted Poisson, and qj would be zero-truncated Poisson, not 1 + Poisson. Eqs. 10 and 13 share a functional form with Eq. 18, not a derivation.
Part IVEstimation, identification, inference

15The objective

Index trips by v = 1…V with observed basket ℬv and descriptor 𝒢v. Write 𝒮v for the sampled category set of §17.2. The four components, each a sum over the trips in a batch ℬ𝒶𝓉 of size M:

ℓinc = ∑v C|𝒮v| ∑c∈𝒮v log P(yc)  [Eq. 9]     ℓbrd = ∑v ∑c:yc=1 log P(kc)  [Eq. 10]
(19)
ℓitem = ∑v ∑j∈Sv log P(j | Sv\{j}, 𝒦v)  [Eq. 15]     ℓqty = ∑v ∑j∈Sv log P(qj)  [Eq. 13]
(20)
ℒ = ℓitem + wqℓqty + wcℓinc + wbℓbrd − τrepr‖Θrepr‖² − τinc‖Θinc‖² − τprice‖Θprice‖²
(21)

ℓitem is Besag's pseudo-likelihood for Eq. 12: the sum of one-position conditionals, exact because Eq. 15 is exact. Three penalty blocks, not two:

blockmemberscoefficient
Θreprα, θ, ψ, μ, δ, ζ, ξ, wloy, wfrq, ψqτrepr = 10−2/1500
Θinccu, cc, csτinc = 10−4/1500
Θpriceγ, β, γq, βqτprice = 10−4/1500

Θprice is separate because shrinking it at the representation block's rate biases the elasticity toward zero, and the elasticity is the estimand.

Θinc must be separate, and this is not a tuning preference

An earlier version of this document put cu, cc, cs inside Θrepr under a single τ. Fitted that way, cu and cc collapse to an effective rank of about 5 and 4 out of K = 64 — 144,512 parameters delivering roughly one number per household times one per category, which cannot express "buys nappies, never cat food". Averaged over all dimensions the data gradient beats the penalty by 5.8–17×, but that average is carried by the one direction that is learning; on the rest the data gradient is near zero and the penalty is the only force. Separating the block raises the effective ranks to about 32 and 24 and improves incidence NLL 0.1249 → 0.1199 against a 0.1132 oracle floor. Eq. 21 with two blocks specifies a model that demonstrably underfits.

Normalisation convention. Each of the four components is evaluated as a mean over its own terms — ℓitem and ℓqty over purchase rows, ℓinc over sampled (trip, category) pairs, ℓbrd over the sampled pairs with yc = 1 — rather than as the sums written in Eqs. 19–20. For ℓqty this is exact, since it shares ℓitem's terms; for ℓbrd it is a factor of about 0.83; for ℓinc it is a factor of about 24.5, which is large enough to change the estimand and is treated below. Defaults: wq = wc = wb = 1, M = 192 baskets, Adam at 0.005 annealed by cosine.

Eq. 21 at wc = 1 is not what the code maximises

The C/|𝒮v| factor in Eq. 19 makes ℓinc an unbiased estimate of the log-likelihood over all C categories, so at w = 1 Eq. 21 is the total log-likelihood and ℓinc carries M·C ≈ 36,096 terms against ℓitem's ≈ 1,476. The implementation instead takes a plain mean of each component over its own term count, which weights incidence ≈ 24.5× lower than Eq. 21 at wc = 1. Every fitted result in this repository is from the reweighted objective.

Both were fitted, and the difference is not cosmetic. Refitting at wc = 24.46, identical in every other respect:

Eq. 21 as written buys 0.0035 nats of incidence likelihood and pays for it with 63% of the price coefficient — a fall of roughly 42 refit standard deviations — while item likelihood also gets slightly worse. The mechanism is mechanical: γ and β enter b, b enters IV, and IV enters incidence, so up-weighting entry 24× pulls the shared price parameters toward what category entry wants, while the item head — which carries 81% of the own-price elasticity through the allocation margin — is correspondingly down-weighted.

Neither weighting is a priori correct; §16 notes that any w ≠ 1 forfeits the pseudo-likelihood consistency argument, and wc = 24.46 forfeits it no less than wc = 1. What can be said is that the implemented convention is the one every result here was produced under, and that it preserves the estimand. Eq. 21 is therefore stated as the code computes it, with this discrepancy recorded rather than silently resolved.

16What consistency does and does not hold

The textbook pseudo-likelihood guarantee does not apply here

Two obstructions, both structural.

Reweighting. Besag's consistency is for the maximiser of ℓitem alone. Eq. 21 maximises a weighted sum of four log-likelihoods; unless wq = wc = wb = 1 and the components share no parameters, its maximiser is not an M-estimator for the parameters of any one of them. They do share parameters — α and γ,β appear in all four — so the weights are not innocuous even at 1.

Incidental parameters. θi, γi, cui, hi, γqi are per-household: 2,066 households, ~145 parameters each, with a bounded number of trips per household. Classical consistency requires the parameter count fixed as the sample grows. This is the Neyman–Scott regime, in which the common parameters are generally inconsistent as well.

What can honestly be claimed: Eq. 21 is a penalised M-estimator whose population objective is maximised at the truth when w = 1 and penalties vanish. Consistency of κ and of γ⊤β requires either an argument that the household parameters are ancillary, or a fixed-effects treatment, or an explicit prior. None is currently supplied.

17The sampled estimators

17.1The inclusive value

Sample m of the Nc stocked products uniformly with replacement:

ÎVict = log( ∑r=1..m ebijrt ) + log Ncm,    m = 32
(22)

The sum is unbiased: 𝔼[(Nc/m)∑reb] = ∑jeb. Its logarithm is biased low by Jensen.

The damage is attenuation of κ, not a level shift

Writing ÎV = IV + e, the estimator enters Eq. 6 as a regressor multiplied by the estimand κc. Noise in such a regressor produces classical attenuation: κ̂ is biased toward zero by roughly Var(IV)/(Var(IV) + Var(e)). Absorbing 𝔼[e] into c0c removes the mean and does nothing about the variance. Further, IV sits inside the nonlinear cloglog of Eq. 9, so 𝔼[∇ℓ] ≠ ∇ℓ(𝔼[·]) — the gradient is biased, and no intercept absorbs a biased gradient. Var(e) falls as 1/m; m = 32 against Nc up to 225 is not obviously enough. Neither the attenuation factor nor its sensitivity to m is currently measured.

17.2Category sampling

Eq. 9 is a Bernoulli over 188 categories per trip, of which 6.12 are entered. Sixteen are sampled uniformly, without replacement, and ℓinc is rescaled by C/|𝒮v| (Eq. 19) so the sampled sum is an unbiased estimate of the full one.

Case-control sampling — a few entered, a few not — is rejected for a reason specific to the link. The standard log(π1/π0) intercept correction is a logit result (Prentice–Pyke): only for the logistic link does outcome-dependent sampling shift the intercept alone and leave the slopes intact. Eq. 9 is cloglog, for which no such invariance holds — outcome-dependent sampling distorts the slopes too, including κ, and cannot be repaired by an offset. Uniform sampling avoids the question.

An earlier version of this document argued instead that case-control biases the term κc(IV − IVc) because its sample mean differs from its population mean. That argument is wrong twice over: a difference in a regressor's sample mean does not by itself bias an M-estimator, and IVc is a fixed constant, which §17.3 correctly says is absorbed by c0c.

17.3The frozen reference

IVc is fixed early in fitting to the mean of ÎVict over a uniform category sample. Being a fixed per-category constant it is absorbed by c0c and does not change the fit; freezing guarantees that training and generation subtract the same thing. It is a distinct object from Eq. 22, which is a per-trip random quantity.

18Identification

Four invariances, none previously stated.

  1. Rotation. b contains θi⊤αj and E contains αj⊤αk; both are invariant under α → Rα, θ → Rθ for any R ∈ O(K). With K = 64 that is a K(K−1)/2 = 2,016-dimensional indeterminacy. Only the Gram matrices are identified. Any reported embedding geometry must be rotation-invariant, or the rotation must be fixed by a convention that is stated.
  2. Scale. Under α → cα, θ → θ/c the taste term is invariant but the interaction scales by c², so the scale of α is pinned — by the interaction, not by the taste term.
  3. Price sign and rotation. γi⊤βj is invariant under O(Kp) and under (γ,β) → (−γ,−β). Only the product is identified, and nothing constrains it to be positive, so the model admits upward-sloping demand for any (i, j) pair. Reported elasticities are meaningful only in aggregate unless a sign restriction is imposed.
  4. Category levels. This is the consequential one. A−j ⊆ 𝒞s(c(j)), so ℓitem only ever compares products within a category: adding a constant to bijt for all j in one category leaves every Eq. 15 unchanged. The category level of b is therefore identified only through IV in Eq. 6 — that is, through the noisy, Jensen-biased Eq. 22, whose bias §17.1 absorbs into c0c, which is the same parameter the category level lives in. The identification of category levels and the correction of the IV bias compete for one parameter.

19Inference

The estimand is the price elasticity (Appendix C) and κ. Neither is a maximum-likelihood estimate, so the inverse Hessian is not a valid variance.

  • Sandwich. For a pseudo-likelihood the asymptotic variance is H−1VH−1 with H the expected Hessian and V the variance of the score. Because ℓitem sums n dependent conditionals per basket, V must be estimated with the basket — not the position — as the independent unit.
  • Cluster. Trips repeat within households, and household parameters are estimated from those same trips, so the resampling unit is the household.
  • Practical route. A household-level block bootstrap over the whole pipeline, refitting each replicate, is the only procedure that also propagates the §17.1 attenuation and the §16 reweighting. It is expensive and is what the estimand requires.

No uncertainty is currently reported for any quantity in this specification. Until it is, differences between model variants cannot be called real.

BConstruction of 𝒢

B.1Δlog p

Price. The loyalty price a card holder faces: (SALES_VALUE − COUPON_MATCH_DISC)/QUANTITY. The coupon match is a manufacturer refund, not a price the shopper responded to. The paid price is not used: two shoppers at one shelf with different coupons would otherwise face different prices for the same product on the same day.

Per product-day. The median over that day's purchase lines. 24.7% of the J × D grid is observed; missing days carry the last observed price forward, and days before a product's first sale carry the first observed price backward.

Centring. With 𝒯 the training days, Δlog pjt = log pjt − |𝒯|−1∑t′∈𝒯 log pjt′, where log p = log max(price, 10−3).

Two properties of this covariate to keep in view

Three quarters of it is imputed. With 24.7% of the grid observed, most values of Δlog p are a carried-forward constant, and Δlog p is exactly zero wherever the carried price equals the training mean. Price variation is therefore concentrated in a minority of product-days, and any elasticity is estimated off that minority.

The centring looks ahead within training. The mean runs over all of 𝒯, including days after t, as does gsub(j) in B.3. This is not test leakage — 𝒯 excludes held-out days — but it does mean a training-day covariate uses information from later training days, which matters if the series is used sequentially.

Store deviation. djsw = log p̃jsw − log p̃jw, store median against chain median that week, kept where |d| > 10−6 — 244,880 cells, 0.53% of the grid; zero elsewhere. The model reads Δlog pjst = Δlog pjt + djsw.

B.2𝒞s(c)

Product j ∈ 𝒞s(c(j)) if store s sold it at least once in the training window. Covers 50.9% of the product × store grid.

The choice set is constructed from the choices

Defining assortment by "was sold" is selection on the outcome, not merely a stale proxy. A product stocked but never bought is deleted from the denominator of Eq. 15 and from the sum in Eq. 6. Both effects run one way: fitted choice probabilities are inflated, and IV is inflated — and IV multiplied by κ is the estimand. An assortment measure independent of purchases, or a sensitivity analysis over the inclusion threshold, is needed before κ is interpreted.

Why the unstocked-pair rule turns out not to matter

Refitting with the rule obeyed, identical in every other respect, moves held-out item likelihood −2.5118 → −2.5091 (inside refit noise), the price coefficient +0.695 → +0.698, κ 0.872 → 0.872, and generated categories per basket 5.60 → 5.62 against a real 6.38. Nothing moves.

The reason is §18's fourth invariance. Removing 9.46% of forced zeros should raise the fitted entry propensity, and the parameters do move — the frozen reference IVc falls from 0.264 to 0.057 while c0 barely shifts — but only the combination c0c + κc(IV − IVc) is identified, and it is unchanged. Mean generated categories is ∑cP(yc = 1), so its agreement to 0.02 categories is direct evidence that the predicted entry probabilities are the same function. The base-rate distortion is real and is absorbed whole by a reparameterisation.

Two consequences worth keeping. The rule is still the right specification — it costs nothing and removes a term that carries no information — but it is not a latent bug, and it is not the explanation for the generator emitting 5.6 categories against a real 6.4. That shortfall remains unexplained.
