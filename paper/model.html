Specification

A retail basket model: one energy, an exact normaliser, and what it costs

A probabilistic model of supermarket baskets whose normaliser is computable in closed form up to a low-dimensional integral, so that a whole basket can be scored, sampled and differentiated with respect to price. This document states the model, proves the results that make it usable, and reports what has been measured on dunnhumby's Complete Journey — including where the model is beaten.

How to read this

  • Derived — follows from stated assumptions, with the derivation given.
  • Verified — checked numerically against brute-force enumeration, with the residual quoted and the script named.
  • Measured — a number from the data or a fitted model.
  • Choices with no derivation behind them are marked in amber.

Every equation states what scripts/v3/ computes. Where the code and this document have disagreed, the disagreement produced a bug; §22 lists the ones found so far.

Part I
The model

1The model in one page

A trip is a shopping occasion by household i at store s on day t. Its outcome is a set of products S drawn from the store's assortment 𝒜s, plus a unit count qj ≥ 1 for each product in it.

Every product carries a standalone value bijt — a linear index in household taste, price, promotion, seasonality and store (Eq. 2). A basket's plausibility is then one number: products are not chosen one at a time and not in stages, the whole set is scored at once.

E(S) = ∑j ∈ S bijt  +  ∑{j,k} ⊆ S φj⊤φk  −  ∑c ρc nc(nc − 1)2  −  ρ0( n )
P( S ) = exp E(S) / Z
(1)
termwhat it encodesfree to be
∑j∈S bjis this product attractive to this household today, on its ownanything a linear index can say
∑{j,k}⊆S φj⊤φkdo these two products belong together — complementarity, within a category and acrosssymmetric, rank Kz, positive semi-definite

The normaliser sums over every subset of the assortment — about 101632 of them at the median store, which carries 5,420 products. Theorem 1 collapses that to a Kz-dimensional Gaussian integral of a product of one-dimensional polynomials, computable to machine precision. Everything else follows from that one fact: an exact likelihood, exact sampling with no Markov chain, and exact derivatives of any purchase probability with respect to any price.

1.1What the model deliberately does not have

  • No composition stage. Which categories are entered, how many products from each, and how large the basket is are all consequences of E. An interaction that cannot change what you enter the aisle for is not modelling complementarity.
  • No sequence, no ordering. A basket is a set and receives one probability, not one per order in which it might have been assembled.
  • No nesting coefficient and no estimated inclusive value. The category-level quantities that would need them are derivatives of log Z, available in closed form (Eq. 8). §13 shows the price of this: the category-entry response becomes an identity rather than a free parameter.
  • No latent quantity reconstructed and then conditioned on. Anything unobserved is either integrated out exactly — the basket effect z — or defined from observables. This is Axiom O (§9), and it is why there is no inventory variable.

2.1The assortment, and why the obvious definition is wrong

The support of Eq. 1 is the store's assortment, so defining it is a modelling decision rather than bookkeeping. The obvious definition — store s carries product j if it sold j during training — is not merely selection on the outcome. It is measurably wrong as a description of availability:

splitpurchase lines outside that supporttrips touched
validation20,396 of 134,665  (15.2%)8,312 of 17,113  (48.6%)
test35,235 of 202,703  (17.4%)12,798 of 24,768  (51.7%)

Half of all held-out baskets contain something the store "does not carry." Those baskets lie outside their own support and have likelihood zero, so no model can be evaluated on them. Adding the offending pairs would leak held-out purchases into the support definition, so that is not available either.

The definition used is training-only and strictly coarser:

j ∈ 𝒜s  ⟺  s sold something from category c(j) in training,  and  j was sold anywhere in the chain in training
(A)

Store-specific at the category level, chain-level within a category. Violations fall to 0.00% of training lines, 1.23% of validation and 2.39% of test, and the remainder are dropped and reported rather than absorbed. This expands the support — 601,431 (store, product) pairs, 95.9% of the grid, median 5,420 products per store against 3,401 under the old definition — which makes the likelihood harder, so it is conservative for anything scored on it.

with w = (WEEK_NO − 1) mod 52 the week of year and xrec the four recency functions of §9.1 — an indicator for no earlier purchase and three decays in the days since the household last bought that sub-commodity, one of them scaled by the sub-commodity's own median repurchase gap.

Measured: what the recency block is worth

Two fits differing only in this term, matched in every other respect. The recency block leads at every one of eight checkpoints and the lead grows: 1.08, 1.44, 1.60, 1.80, 1.59, 1.77, 1.76 nats per basket. It is the largest single contribution of any covariate block, and it had been specified and left unimplemented.

One function, and nothing else may compute an item value

Eq. 2 is evaluated by a single routine, b_at(items, trips, ctx), called both where the normaliser scores the assortment and where the energy scores the observed basket. They were once separate code paths and drifted: the normaliser applied price, promotion, seasonality and store while the energy applied only taste, so E(S) and log Z valued the same product differently and the gap was free reward for the optimiser. A regression check requires the value at a purchased line to equal the value at that product's assortment slot; it reads 0.0 after the fix and 0.071 on the old path.

4The normaliser

Theorem 1 — the normaliser

Write b̃j = bijt − ½‖φj‖² and wj(z) = exp(b̃j + z⊤φj). Let er be the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree r, and define the per-category generating polynomial

Gc(u; z) = ∑r=0min(Nsc, R) exp( −ρc r(r−1)2 )   er( { wj(z) }j ∈ 𝒞s(c) )   ur
(3)

Let An(z) be the coefficients of ∏c Gc(u; z) = ∑n An(z) un. Then

Z = 𝔼z ~ N(0, IKz) [ ∑n=0nmax e−ρ0(n) An(z) ]
(4)

Proof. Three steps. Complete the square: with vS = ∑j∈Sφj, ∑{j,k}⊆Sφj⊤φk = ½(‖vS‖² − ∑j∈S‖φj‖²), because ‖vS‖² counts each unordered pair twice and each diagonal once; absorbing the diagonal into b̃ leaves e½‖vS‖². Hubbard–Stratonovich: the Gaussian moment generating function gives 𝔼z[ez⊤v] = e½‖v‖², and exchanging the finite sum over subsets with the expectation leaves a summand that is a product over j ∈ S — the coupling between products has gone, traded for a Kz-dimensional integral. Group by counts: the subsets with a given count vector contribute ∏cerc(w(c)) by the definition of er, and the polynomial variable u carries the constraint ∑crc = n. ∎

Verified

verify_normaliser.py enumerates all 2J subsets for five configurations and compares log Z against Eq. 4 by quadrature: worst relative error 3.9 × 10−15. verify_size_term.py repeats it with a free-form ρ0: 2.0 × 10−15. Both at double-precision round-off.

4.1Three corollaries

Corollary 1 — the empty basket, and exact conditioning

E(∅) = 0, so P(S = ∅) = 1/Z exactly. A trip is observed only because something was bought, and the conditional normaliser is therefore Z − 1.

It must be computed by dropping the n = 0 term from Eq. 4, not by subtracting 1 afterwards. The empty basket contributes exactly 1 to the integrand for every z, so removing it is exact; forming exp(log Z) − 1 instead subtracts two nearly equal numbers whenever Z is close to 1, which is precisely the regime a diverging fit collapses into. Verified: the two agree to 4 × 10−6 where both are valid.

Corollary 2 — inclusion probabilities and the gradient

log Z is the cumulant generating function of the purchase indicator x in the natural parameter b:

∂ log Z∂ bj = 𝔼[xj] = P(j ∈ S),    ∂² log Z∂ bj∂ bk = Cov(xj, xk)
(5)

Conditionally on z, with f(z) = ∑ne−ρ0(n)An(z),

P(j ∈ S | z) = ∂ log f(z)∂ log wj = ∑r P(nc = r | z) · wj er−1(w(c) \ wj)er(w(c))
(6)

With ρ0 active the count law P(nc=r|z) is not Gc's own coefficient ratio but its leave-one-out marginal, ∝ Gc[r]∑me−ρ0(r+m)B(−c)m, obtainable for all categories from the prefix and suffix products the sampler already builds. Verified against enumeration to 3.7 × 10−15, and the identity confirmed by finite differences to 7 × 10−11.

Corollary 3 — sampling, exact given z

  1. Draw z from its posterior by importance resampling.
  2. Draw the total size n ∝ e−ρ0(n)An(z).
  3. Draw the split (rc) given n by a backward pass over the suffix products.
  4. Draw rc products per category by the sequential suffix-ESP algorithm, exact in O(Nsc rc).

Steps 2–4 are exact; step 1 is exact only as the proposal count grows, with error governed by the effective sample size. No Gibbs sweeps, no burn-in, no mixing diagnostic. Verified: frequencies over 40,000 draws match the enumerated law to total variation 0.018–0.103 against noise floors of 0.017–0.103, on all nine configurations.

5The nesting

Theorem 1 is usually read as a computational device. It is also the statement that this is a nested model, and the nesting is worth writing out because it is derived rather than assumed. Conditionally on z, Eq. 1 factorises exactly:

P( S | z ) = P( n | z )  ·  P( (rc) | n, z )  ·  ∏c P( Sc | rc, z )
(7)
levelwhat is chosenits inclusive value
1 — sizehow many lines in totallog f(z)
2 — allocationhow those lines split across categorieslog An(z)
3 — selectionwhich products within a categorylog erc(w(c))

Three differences from a nested logit, and they are the point. No dissimilarity parameters — the coefficients on the inclusive values are all 1, because the three levels are a factorisation of one joint rather than three models joined by hand. The nests are not a partition of a single choice, so level 3 is a subset choice (er) rather than a softmax; setting R = 1 collapses it to the familiar log-sum-exp. The correlation lives in z and is integrated out exactly rather than approximated by correlated errors.

6Units

Assumption U, and what it yields

If the energy does not depend on q, then P(S, q) ∝ exp E(S) ∏j∈Shj(qj) with each hj normalised over q ≥ 1, and summing q out leaves exactly P(S). So

P(S, q) = P(S) ∏j ∈ S P(qj | j ∈ S)
(8)

is derived from Assumption U, not assumed as an ordering of decisions. What it rules out is that buying three of something changes which other products appear.

The units law is a shifted negative binomial with its own price index:

P(qj = ν | j ∈ S) = NBr( ν − 1 ; Λqtyij ),    log Λqtyij = aj − ( γq⊤iβqj ) Δlog pjst
(9)

The negative binomial rather than a Poisson is forced by the data, not chosen: measured on training rows, P(q=1) = 0.7768, mean 1.3564, variance 0.8387 — so q is under-dispersed (0.62) while q − 1 is over-dispersed (2.35), which no one-parameter Poisson can produce.

Why units are not optional

Revenue is ∑ pjqj. A model of the set alone cannot price a promotion, so a simulator that omits Eq. 9 is not a partial environment but an unusable one. This was deferred once and should not have been.

7Truncations, and why they are restrictions rather than approximations

R caps the products taken from one category and nmax caps the basket. Both restrict the support of Eq. 1, so the energy and the normaliser must agree about membership — a basket the normaliser does not sum over must not be scored by the energy, and clamping it to the cap silently relabels it as in-support.

8The conditionals

The joint is written first and the conditionals read off, which is the direction that cannot go wrong. For j ∉ S:

log P(S ∪ {j})P(S) = bijt + ∑k ∈ S φj⊤φk − ρc(j) nc(j)(S) − [ ρ0(n+1) − ρ0(n) ]
(10)

The product's own value; the total pull of what is already there; a penalty in how many of the same category are present; and the marginal cost of one more line. Nothing here invokes Hammersley–Clifford or a positivity condition — the joint is written down and shown to be summable, and the conditionals are consequences.

9The state, and where every conditioning variable comes from

Axiom S — closure

Every quantity the basket law conditions on is either an exogenous input supplied from outside — calendar, and the retailer's actions — or a deterministic function of the household's own simulated history. Nothing is a lookup frozen at the training window.

Axiom O — provenance

A quantity may enter in exactly one of three ways: observed; latent and integrated out, as z is in Eq. 4; or defined as a stated function of observables. What is forbidden is a fourth: reconstructing an unobserved quantity from a recursion whose rate and initial condition are not identified, then conditioning on the reconstruction as though it were observed.

Axiom O exists because an earlier draft violated it, with inventory

dunnhumby contains no inventory data. Purchases are the inflow; consumption is never observed; the opening stock is never observed. A per-category inventory recursion therefore has an unidentified rate and initial condition, both "identified" only through the functional form of the model they feed. It is also a belief rather than a state — if stock is latent the Markov state is the posterior over it and the control problem is a POMDP — and it was inconsistent with a model that integrates z out exactly.

What it was there to do is kept as a defined quantity: with Δtic the days since the household last bought from category c, Qic the units taken then, and gc the category's median repurchase gap,

xsupict = Qic − Δticgc
(11)

All three ingredients are observed and there is no free rate. Measured: the only new content beyond recency is Q, and it is real but weak — within (household, category), one extra unit lengthens the next gap by 0.97% (β = +0.0097, t = +8.2, household-clustered, 797,533 occasions), worth +0.000387 nats held out. Forward-buying is representable, and it is small.

Part II
Why this parameterisation and not another

10The response identity

Proposition 1 — the basket-size response is a covariance

Under b → b + εd,

d 𝔼[n]d ε = Cov( n , d⊤x ),    and for a uniform shift   d 𝔼[n]d δ = Var( n )
(12)

Proof. Immediate from Corollary 2. ∎ Verified by finite differences against enumeration: relative error 1.1–1.5 × 10−10.

A uniform proportional price change does not produce a uniform shift in b, since γi⊤βj varies by product. What carries the argument generally is Cauchy–Schwarz: |Cov(n, d⊤x)| ≤ sd(n)·sd(d⊤x). So under-producing the basket-size variance caps the achievable aggregate price response whatever the loadings look like. Distributional fidelity and counterfactual validity are the same property here, not two desiderata.

Measured: basket size has mean 7.849 and variance 83.039, a dispersion index of 10.58. Conditioning on household, day-of-week and inter-trip gap explains 59% of that variance, leaving a residual dispersion of 4.35 — which is what a latent mechanism must supply.

11What a Gaussian latent can and cannot reach

Proposition 2 — the reach of a real Gaussian latent

Any model of the form P(S) ∝ ∫∏j∈Swj(z)dμ(z) with μ Gaussian has pairwise interaction exactly W = ΦΦ⊤ ⪰ 0. Hence every product attracts itself (‖φj‖² ≥ 0), products aligned in φ attract, and total repulsion is bounded below by −½∑j∈S‖φj‖². Splitting W = Φ+Φ+⊤ − Φ−Φ−⊤ would require 𝔼[eiz⊤v] = e−½‖v‖², an oscillatory integrand whose Monte Carlo variance grows exponentially. That route is closed.

This is why the within-category term is enumerated rather than embedded. −ρcC(nc,2) adds −ρc(11⊤−I) to W, itself indefinite, so the total interaction is indefinite — unreachable by any Gaussian latent, and summed over exactly by grouping on nc at cost O(NscR). The latent carries the attractive, low-rank, cross-category structure; the enumeration carries the repulsive, structured, within-category part.

Measured, and it is the clearest empirical support for this section

A symmetric low-rank DPP scores det(LS) with L ⪰ 0 — repulsion only, the mirror image of Eq. 1's limitation. Fitted on the same data with the same covariates it reaches −60.88 per basket, ten nats below a zero-parameter frequency baseline, and it had converged rather than stalled. A nonsymmetric DPP, whose skew part is exactly what lets a determinantal model express attraction, does no better: −61.20. So the failure is not about symmetry; a determinantal kernel is the wrong object for grocery baskets, which are dominated by complementarity.

12Stability, and why the size potential is forced

Write the log integrand of Eq. 4 as F(z) = −½‖z‖² + log f(z). By Corollary 2 applied conditionally,

∇F(z) = −z + ∑j πj(z) φj,    ∇²F(z) = −I + Λ(z),    Λ(z) := Cov( ∑j φj xj | z ) ⪰ 0
(13)

Proposition 3 — stability

If supz λmax(Λ(z)) < 1 then F is strictly concave, the mode equation z = ∑jπj(z)φj has a unique solution, the fixed-point iteration is a global contraction, and the Laplace proposal is well defined. The condition is global: evaluating λmax at one mode is necessary, not sufficient, since after a bifurcation each mode is locally stable. The operational diagnostic is multi-start plus the importance-sampling effective sample size.

Proposition 3b — the dispersion–stability bound

At Laplace order, with Λ0 = Var(n|z⋆) and u = Cov(n, vS|z⋆),

Var(n) ≤ Λ01 − λmax(Λ)   ⟺   λmax(Λ) ≥ 1 − Var(n | z⋆)Var(n)
(14)

Proof. With e = u/‖u‖, u⊤(I−Λ)−1u ≤ ‖u‖²/(1−λmax), and by Cauchy–Schwarz on the conditional covariance ‖u‖² ≤ Var(n|z⋆)·e⊤Λe ≤ Λ0λmax. Adding Λ0 gives the bound. ∎ Verified on nine instances at Kz = 1, 2, 3 crossed with ρ0 absent, convex and free-form: zero violations.

Note the conditional variance. Writing Λ0 = ∑π(1−π) instead is valid only when the indicators are conditionally independent — which ρc and ρ0 destroy. With φ = 0, so λmax = 0 exactly, a free ρ0 can give Var(n) = 36 against ∑π(1−π) = 3: the wrong form is violated twelve-fold.

12.1What that forces

Specialise to ρc = ρ0 = 0, where the indicators are conditionally independent and Λ0 ≈ 𝔼[n]. Then Eq. 14 becomes λmax ≥ 1 − 1/dispersion, and that sub-model is exactly a basket model whose only interaction is a low-rank Gaussian latent — the natural thing to write down. On this data it would have to run at λmax ≥ 0.906 raw, or ≥ 0.770 against the residual dispersion of 4.35. Neither is safe: in enumerable toys the Laplace variance formula is already 26% wrong at λ = 0.31 and the system bifurcates by 0.53, with ESS collapsing 0.878 → 0.254 → 0.001.

The escape is to raise Var(n|z⋆) rather than λ, which is what a free ρ0 does directly. Measured: a convex ρ0 in the aligned worst case delays the bifurcation from a loading of 0.60 to 0.75 without abolishing it, and at a fixed loading of 0.60 turns two modes and 0.68 nats of error into one mode and 0.003.

Measured on real data, and the reason the estimator is cheap

The fitted model runs at λmax between 0.10 and 0.29 with no drift, holding ‖φ‖ ≤ 0.15. That cap was calibrated rather than guessed: a cap of 0.35 measures λmax = 0.668, near the critical point, and 0.50 measures 1.658, outright unstable.

13Where the nesting went, and what it costs

Proposition 5 — the category-entry response is not a free parameter

Conditionally on z, shifting every product value in category c by δ,

d log P(nc ≥ 1)d δ = 𝔼[r | r ≥ 1] − 𝔼[r] = 𝔼[r | r ≥ 1] · ( 1 − P(nc ≥ 1) )
(15)

Proof. er is homogeneous of degree r, so a shift multiplies the weight at r by erδ; differentiating log∑r≥1 − log∑r≥0 gives the two means. ∎ Verified against enumeration on five configurations spanning N = 6 to 20 and both signs of ρc: relative error 1–4 × 10−11.

This is the sharpest bet the architecture makes

Both quantities in Eq. 15 are measured, not fitted: 𝔼[r|r≥1] = 1.2844 and the per-category entry rate is 0.0323. So the model pins the category-entry response at 1.243, with no parameter free to move it. A nested model that estimates this quantity instead has reported values below 1. This model cannot absorb that difference — which is the price of removing a free parameter: more falsifiable, and more fragile. The test is a held-out category-incidence comparison, and it has not been run.

Part III
Estimation

14The likelihood

ℓv(Θ) = E(Sv) − log( Z − 1 ) + ∑j ∈ Sv log P( qj | j ∈ Sv )
(16)

One objective; no component weights to choose. log(Z−1) is computed by Corollary 1's exact route. The estimator is Monte Carlo maximum likelihood, and its properties are known rather than assumed: Ẑ is unbiased for Z, so by Jensen 𝔼[log Ẑ] ≤ log Z and the estimated log-likelihood is an upper bound in expectation — the unsafe direction, since the optimiser gains wherever log Z is underestimated. The bias is ≈ −(1/D)(1/ESS − 1)/2, and ESS is logged every iteration because it is the diagnostic that detects the failure mode that would make it large.

15The estimator, and why a cheap proposal is legitimate

Z is estimated by importance sampling from a Gaussian centred at the mode of Eq. 13. The proposal covariance is the identity, and the mode is located in three fixed-point steps.

Fitting the curvature by finite differences cost 2Kz gradient evaluations — 65% of every call — and buys 0.002 nats. This is not a corner cut, it follows from Proposition 3. The posterior of z is N(0,I) tilted by Λ, so the exact covariance is (I−Λ)−1; at the λmax ≈ 0.1–0.3 the stability condition requires, that lies between I and about 1.4I. The condition that makes the estimator correct is the condition that makes fitting its covariance unnecessary. The mode itself still matters: dropping it costs ESS and an order of magnitude.

16Numerical requirements

The elementary symmetric polynomials must come from the recursion, not from power sums

Newton's identities express er as an alternating sum of power sums, which is attractive because a scatter-add needs no per-row loop over a ragged axis. It is numerically unusable above R ≈ 4: at order 12 and 23 the terms reach 1030 and cancel.

17The optimiser was the binding constraint, not the model

Every fit reported here before this section used a constant learning rate, and every one of them appeared to converge at roughly half an epoch. That appearance was false. The training loss stops falling at about a third of an epoch and then oscillates, while held-out likelihood swings across a 1.4-nat band with no trend:

That is a step size too large for the stage, not a model at capacity. Resuming from the 0.52-epoch checkpoint with the rate dropped from 0.005 to 0.0006 under cosine decay broke the floor within 150 iterations: training loss 51.70 → 50.61 and held-out −45.54 → −44.11, a gain of 1.43 nats after 1,200 iterations of no progress.

What this invalidated

Three separate "convergences" reported earlier were the optimiser stalling. Worse for the comparison: every baseline was trained the same way, with the same optimiser and the same constant rate, so any margin measured between them was measuring a shared defect. Every number in §19 is therefore re-run with cosine decay at matched compute, and figures from before that change are not comparable to figures after it.

This is the second time the same error appeared in a different guise. The first was compute: baselines trained for 0.09 epochs against this model's 0.46, which moved SHOPPER by 2.99 nats and the multinomial by 4.64 once corrected. Both times the fault was holding one model to a standard the others were not held to.

18Cost, and what consistency does not hold

Measured at R = 23, nmax = 120, Kz = 12, batch 24, 16 draws: 3.5 s per iteration, 130,000 assortment slots per batch, 514,941 parameters, and about three hours for 3,000 iterations.

What can honestly be claimed: Eq. 16 is a penalised M-estimator whose population objective is maximised at the truth as D → ∞, with the household parameters treated as fixed effects and the number of trips per household growing. Neither condition holds here. θi, γi and their relatives are per household — 2,066 households seen a mean of 76 times each — which is the Neyman–Scott regime, in which the common parameters are generally inconsistent too. Weight decay shrinks but does not solve it. Any interval must come from a household block bootstrap over refits, which has not been run.

The honest reading

The multinomial is this model with φ = 0, ρc = 0 and ρ0 set to the empirical size law — a nested special case. So the gap to it, currently about 1.4 nats, is what the interaction buys once basket size is handed over for free. Against independent Bernoulli the margin looks far larger, but Bernoulli has no notion of size at all, which is an easy thing to beat and not evidence about the interaction.

SHOPPER is ahead. Its number is a lower bound, since its ordering estimator is biased low, so the true gap is at least what is shown. This model is still training and improving; the ranking is not settled, but there is no basis for claiming it wins.

21What is not established

  • The causal layer. Nothing has been estimated. The shelf-price coefficient is not identified from this data by any modelling choice — the retailer sets the treatment and observes the shock, and three quarters of the price series is imputed. Coupon eligibility is the one credible contrast, and it identifies an intent-to-treat effect, which is the right estimand for targeting.
  • The environment. Trip timing is specified and unimplemented. By the simulation lemma, an H-step policy value obeys |ΔV| ≤ H²Rmaxε with ε the per-step transition error; at the accuracy this model class reaches, that bound is vacuous for twelve-step evaluation. The defensible use is policy search, with off-policy evaluation on logged data for the value.
  • Proposition 5's prediction, which is the architecture's sharpest and most falsifiable claim.
  • Consistency, per §18.
