Basket Model v4

A probabilistic model over grocery baskets: it scores a set of products, answers price counterfactuals, and serves as the environment for coupon and markdown policies. This document specifies the model exactly and records what has been measured about it — including the claims that did not survive.

1 · The model

A basket is a set S of products, not a sequence. Its probability is Gibbs in an energy:

P(S | household h, trip t)  =  exp( E(S) ) / Z

E(S) = sum_{j in S} b_jht                     item value
     + sum_{j<k in S} phi_j' phi_k            complementarity
     - sum_c rho_c * n_c (n_c - 1) / 2         within-category interaction
     - rho_0(n)                                basket-size potential

with n = |S| and n_c the number of items from category c. The item value is additive:

b_jht = lam_j                                  product intercept
      + theta_h' alpha_j                       household taste
      - softplus(gamma_h)' softplus(beta_j) * dlp_jt      price
      + w_dsp_j * display + w_mlr_j * mailer   promotion
      + mu_j' delta_w                          season (week w)
      + zeta_j' xi_s                           store s
      + psi_j' rec_jt                          recency

Three design consequences follow immediately, and every measured strength and weakness traces to one of them.

The size law is separate. rho_0(n) governs how many items a basket holds, independently of which. This is why the model calibrates basket size and price elasticity well: a uniform price shift moves E[n] through Proposition 1, dE[n]/dDelta = Var(n), with no need to re-rank products.

Complementarity is low-rank and positive semidefinite. phi_j' phi_k is a Gram matrix. It expresses products going together; it cannot express broad substitution, which must live in rho_c.

Ranking is normaliser-free. Comparing S ∪ {j} against S ∪ {k} cancels Z entirely, so recommendation costs one forward pass and no sampling. It also cancels rho_0 and every shared item — which is why the model is far better at judging whole baskets than at picking the next item.

3 · The normaliser

Z sums exp(E(S)) over all 25455 subsets. It is made computable by a Gaussian identity (Hubbard–Stratonovich):

exp( ||v||^2 / 2 ) = E_{z ~ N(0, I_Kz)}[ exp(v'z) ],    v = sum_{j in S} phi_j

=>  Z = E_z[ f(z) ],   f(z) = sum_n exp(-rho_0(n)) A_n(w(z))
    w_j(z) = exp( b_j - ||phi_j||^2 / 2 + phi_j' z )

Given z the items are independent, so the sum over subsets factorises and f(z) is computed exactly by an elementary-symmetric-polynomial recursion. The only approximation in the entire model is the outer expectation over a 4-dimensional Gaussian.

Why importance sampling fails, and what c is

As ||z|| grows along a direction u, the dominant basket collects every product whose embedding points that way, so log f(z) ~ c ||z|| with

c = max over unit u of  sum_j max(phi_j' u, 0)

The integrand therefore behaves like exp(c||z|| - ||z||^2/2) and keeps its mass in a shell at radius c. A proposal centred at the mode with standard deviation capped at 4.47 never visits that shell when c is large. At the original Kz = 32, c = 74: the sampler drew at radius ~5 while the mass sat beyond 100.

c is a computational quantity, not a modelling one. z is an integration variable introduced by a Gaussian identity, not a latent with meaning. A large c means the integral is hard, and says nothing about whether the model is sensible.

Deterministic quadrature

A Smolyak sparse grid computes the expectation deterministically — no proposal, no importance weights, no effective sample size, no convergence guard:

A(q,d) = sum_{q-d+1 <= |i| <= q} (-1)^{q-|i|} C(d-1, q-|i|) (U^{i_1} x ... x U^{i_d})

with U^i the probabilists' Gauss–Hermite rule at level i. Weights are signed, so the sum is formed in linear space after factoring out the row maximum. Verified against exact enumeration — the z-integral closes in form, so ground truth needs no sampling at all:

Z = sum_S exp( -rho_0(|S|) + sum_S b_j + sum_{j<k in S} phi_j' phi_k )

The grid has a hard radius — 3.75 at q=7, 4.51 at q=8. The error transitions exactly where c crosses it: at c = 3.27 the error is 0.012 nats; at c = 6.29 it is 0.914. So c is projected below the radius after every step.

Exploiting sparsity

Only products carrying phi depend on z. With a 20-product mask that is 99.6% of the work repeated needlessly at every node, so the elementary-symmetric polynomial over the remainder and the convolution over untouched categories are lifted out of the node loop:

E_c(z) = E_c^{no-phi}  *  E_c^{phi}(z)          per category
A(z)   = A_const       *  A_active(z)           across categories

83.7× faster at a 20-product mask, bit-equal to the dense path (max relative difference 2.3e-16) and gradient-equal on masked-in phi (6.9e-14). 681 nodes now cost less per iteration than 16 sampled draws did.

4 · Training

Maximise held-out set log-likelihood by Adam with a cosine schedule. Because log Z is now exact, the objective is the actual likelihood rather than a biased surrogate.

Penalties

--size-kl
cross-entropy of the model size law to the empirical one
--rkl-w
reverse KL on the size law; the forward CE is blind to a heavy tail
--elast-w
pins the aggregate price elasticity to the measured −0.121
--pool-prod
ridge on the bilinear product, not on its factors

Projections applied after every step

--c-max
scales phi so c stays inside the grid radius
--lam-centre
centres lam, transferring the level to rho_0
--lam-sd-max
caps the spread of lam
centring
delta, xi, theta centred in b_at, structurally
Why penalties act on products, not factors. A squared penalty on each factor of a bilinear term becomes a nuclear-norm penalty on their product — degree 1 — while lam pays a ridge, degree 2. Verified numerically: bilinear cost per unit is flat at 2.204e-4 across ||v|| from 50 to 400, ridge cost per unit-squared flat at 5e-6. Below the crossover the bilinear route is always cheaper, so the intercept migrates into it. This is why raising --pool-ctx never worked: it changes the coefficient, not the exponent.

Gauge fixing

lam_j + mu_j' delta_w has an exact flat direction: lam_j → lam_j + mu_j' a with delta_w → delta_w - a leaves every b unchanged. The likelihood cannot choose where the intercept lives; only the penalties can, and they choose badly. Centring delta, xi and theta removes the freedom. All three are needed — centring only the contextual terms lets the intercept migrate into household taste instead.

5 · Evaluation

Trips are stored in time order, so any prefix is a single week. Every evaluation samples uniformly across a split with a fixed seed. Both validation and test are always reported.

The slice that flattered everything. The training loop scored va[:128] — week 83 alone. Its worst trip had model E[n] = 16.5; a random sample reaches 117. Measured on the prefix, likelihood read −45.6 where a random sample gives −52.1, and the empirical target itself was wrong (7.2 items and variance 67, against a true 8.6 and 89). Fixed by permuting the validation array once with a fixed seed.

Reported at every checkpoint: held-out set log-likelihood; E[n] as both mean and median, because the failure is concentrated in a tail; population Var(n) = E[Var(n|trip)] + Var[E(n|trip)]; aggregate price elasticity; complete-the-basket MRR. Best-likelihood and best-MRR checkpoints are saved separately.

6.8   Choosing Kz

Two independent lines agree. On the s25 mission-mixture truth — outside every entrant's family — Kz=4 recovers 97.9% of the achievable ceiling against 91.9% at Kz=2. And the real co-purchase log-lift spectrum, tested against a matched independence null, has 4–5 strongly separated modes above the noise floor (eigenvalues 100.0, 40.5, 29.5, 28.0, then a plateau at 17–25 against a floor of 13).

7 · Limitations

Complementarity is budget-limited. c must fit inside the quadrature radius, and every product carrying phi spends from the same pot. Twenty products at phi'phi = 0.35, or all 5,455 at 0.0009. Reaching grocery strength (0.92) on 20 products needs ~30,000 nodes at Kz=6. The class expresses complementarity that is strong and narrow or broad and negligible.

Per-product price sensitivity is arbitrary. The aggregate elasticity is pinned and correct; nothing constrains its allocation across products, and beta correlates +0.016 with the empirical per-product response. Trust "a 10% cut grows baskets 1.2%"; do not trust "discount X rather than Y".

Substitution has one scalar per category. phi phi' is PSD, so the negative eigenvalues of the empirical log-lift spectrum (down to −57) must live in rho_c or nowhere.

Test degrades relative to validation. E[n] 9.88 against 8.04 observed on test, versus 8.95 against 7.87 on validation — temporal drift over weeks 91–102.
